## Coding rules

1. Wandering is defined as the act of moving (walking or locomotion in a wheelchair) from place to place with or without a specified course or known direction; it may or may not be aimless and may or may not be driven by confused thoughts or delusional ideas.[^6]
2. If wandering occurred, determine the frequency of the wandering during the 7-day look-back period.[^7]
3. Pacing (repetitive walking with a driven/pressured quality) within a constrained space is not included in wandering.[^8]
4. Wandering may be coded even if the resident is in a locked unit.[^9]
5. Traveling via a planned course to another specific place (such as going to the dining room to eat a meal or to an activity) is not considered wandering.[^10]
6. Resident intent should not be taken into account when coding items in Section E; the section focuses on the resident's actions, not the intent of their behavior.[^11]
7. Wandering is excluded from item E0200C (other behavioral symptoms not directed toward others).[^12]
8. When E0900 is coded 1, 2, or 3, item E1000 (Wandering—Impact) must be answered; when coded 0, skip to E1100.[^13]
9. E0900 is reached from E0300 when E0300 is coded 0 (skip to E0800) — the item follows E0800 in the sequence; the item stem asks "Has the resident wandered?" with codes 0-3.[^14]
