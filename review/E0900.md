## Coding rules

1. Wandering is the act of moving (walking or locomotion in a wheelchair) from place to place with or without a specified course or known direction; it may or may not be aimless, and the resident may have a purpose (e.g., searching) but persists without knowing the exact direction or location of the object, person or place.[^6]
2. Pacing (repetitive walking with a driven/pressured quality) within a constrained space is not included in wandering.[^7]
3. Wandering may be coded even if the resident is in a locked unit.[^8]
4. Traveling via a planned course to another specific place (such as going to the dining room to eat a meal or to an activity) is not considered wandering.[^9]
5. If E0900 is coded 0, skip to E1100, Change in Behavior or Other Symptoms; if coded 1, 2, or 3, proceed to answer E1000, Wandering—Impact.[^10]
6. E1000, Wandering—Impact, is answered only if E0900 was coded 1, 2, or 3.[^11]
7. Item E0200C (other behavioral symptoms not directed toward others) does not include wandering; wandering is captured in E0900.[^12]
8. Resident intent should not be taken into account when coding items in Section E; the section focuses on the resident's actions, not the intent of their behavior.[^13]
