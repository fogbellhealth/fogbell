## Look-back window

**7 days**[^1]

Two look-back periods apply to this section. The active-disease observation window used to determine whether the diagnosis is coded is 7 days (Step 2). A separate 60-day window (Step 1) applies to identification of the physician-documented diagnosis: "The disease conditions in this section require a physician-documented diagnosis ... in the last 60 days" (p. I-8). The 30-day exception stated for I2300 UTI does not apply to I2900. The manual does not state how the 7-day window is counted relative to the ARD on these pages.

> **Note:** the manual states more than one time period for this item. See the *Conflicts* section below.

## Coding rules

1. Code diseases that have a documented diagnosis in the last 60 days and have a direct relationship to the resident's current functional status, cognitive status, mood or behavior status, medical treatments, nursing monitoring, or risk of death during the 7-day look-back period.[^4]
2. The disease conditions in this section require a physician-documented diagnosis (or by a nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or clinical nurse specialist if allowable under state licensure laws) in the last 60 days.[^5]
3. Once a diagnosis is identified, it must be determined whether it is active; active diagnoses have a direct relationship to the resident's current functional, cognitive, or mood or behavior status, medical treatments, nursing monitoring, or risk of death during the 7-day look-back period.[^6]
4. Check off each active disease; check all that apply.[^7]
5. Diseases coded in a category are not limited to the examples listed; the parenthetical examples (for I2900: diabetic retinopathy, nephropathy, neuropathy) are examples only and not an all-inclusive list.[^8]
6. If a disease or condition is not specifically listed among the checkbox items, enter the diagnosis and ICD code in item I8000, Additional active diagnosis.[^9]
7. If the primary medical condition coded in I0020B is diabetes, it must also be included (checked) in items I0100–I8000, Active Diagnoses in the Last 7 Days; diabetes is listed as an example of a Medically Complex Condition (I0020 = 13).[^10]
8. When a Z code is assigned for aftercare following a hospitalization, another diagnosis for the related primary medical condition should be checked in items I0100–I7900 or entered in I8000.[^11]
9. If none of the listed active diagnoses apply within the past 7 days, code I7900, None of the above active diagnoses.[^12]
10. Listing a disease/diagnosis on the resident's medical record problem list is not sufficient for determining active or inactive status.[^13]
11. Ongoing therapy with medications or other interventions to manage a condition that requires monitoring for therapeutic efficacy or for potentially severe side effects in the last 7 days indicates active disease; monitoring of medications for adverse effects alone (usual nursing practice, i.e., management of pharmacotherapy) does not count as monitoring of the underlying disease.[^14]
12. If a diagnosis/problem list is used as a source, only diagnoses confirmed by the physician should be entered.[^15]

## What the chart must show

1. Physician (or NP/PA/CNS if allowable under state licensure laws) documented diagnosis of diabetes mellitus in the medical record within the last 60 days. Medical record sources include progress notes, the most recent history and physical, transfer documents, discharge summaries, and diagnosis/problem list. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^16]
   Required for identification of the diagnosis (Step 1).
2. Diagnoses communicated verbally must be documented in the medical record by the physician. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^17]
3. Diagnostic information, including past history obtained from family members and close contacts, must be documented in the medical record by the physician. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^18]
4. Evidence in the medical record for the last 7 days supporting active status; check transfer documents, physician progress notes, recent history and physical, recent discharge summaries, nursing assessments, nursing care plans, medication sheets, doctor's orders, consults and official diagnostic reports. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^19]
5. Specific documentation by a physician, nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or clinical nurse specialist that the condition is active (e.g., in progress notes, most recent history and physical, transfer notes, hospital discharge summary) is sufficient and requires no additional confirmation. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^20]
   Framed as an indicator that may assist assessors.
6. In the absence of specific documentation that the disease is active: documentation of recent onset or acute exacerbation indicated by a positive study, test or procedure, hospitalization for acute symptoms, and/or recent change in therapy in the last 7 days (sources: radiological reports, hospital discharge summaries, doctor's orders). *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^21]
   Listed as an indicator that may be used to confirm active disease.
7. In the absence of specific documentation that the disease is active: documentation of symptoms and abnormal signs indicating ongoing or decompensated disease in the last 7 days, with the symptom specifically attributed to the disease (sources: radiological reports, nursing assessments and care plans, progress notes). *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^22]
   Listed as an indicator that may be used to confirm active disease.
8. In the absence of specific documentation that the disease is active: documentation of ongoing therapy with medications or other interventions in the last 7 days that requires monitoring for therapeutic efficacy or for potentially severe side effects. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^23]
   Listed as an indicator that may be used to confirm active disease.

## ⚠️ Conflicts — please read carefully

The manual (or two documents) say different things here. Both sides are shown; nothing has been dropped.

> **Two coexisting look-back windows for Section I diagnosis items (60-day identification vs. 7-day active status)**
>
> - *Federal manual:* Diagnosis identification (Step 1) uses a 60-day look-back period.[^24]
> - *Federal manual:* Diagnosis status (active or inactive, Step 2) uses a 7-day look-back period.[^1]
>
> Status: Resolved by the document itself: the two windows are sequential steps — the diagnosis must be physician-documented within 60 days and must be active during the 7-day look-back period. The 7-day window is the assessor's observation window recorded in lookback_days.
