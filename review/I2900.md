## Look-back window

**7 days**[^1]

The section states two look-back periods. The assessor's observation window for determining whether the diagnosis is ACTIVE is 7 days (ARD plus the 6 previous calendar days per the general convention at p. 2-17, "ARD + 6 previous calendar days"). A separate 60-day window applies to Step 1, identification of a physician-documented diagnosis: "Diagnosis identification (Step 1) is a 60-day look-back period." (p. I-8). The stated exception for a 30-day window applies only to Item I2300 UTI, not to I2900.

> **Note:** the manual states more than one time period for this item. See the *Conflicts* section below.

## Coding rules

1. Code I2900 for diabetes mellitus (DM); examples given include diabetic retinopathy, nephropathy, and neuropathy.[^4]
2. I2900 is listed under the Metabolic disease category on the item set, to be checked if it applies ("Check all that apply").[^5]
3. Code diseases that have a documented diagnosis in the last 60 days and have a direct relationship to the resident's current functional status, cognitive status, mood or behavior status, medical treatments, nursing monitoring, or risk of death during the 7-day look-back period.[^6]
4. The disease conditions in this section require a physician-documented diagnosis (or by a nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or clinical nurse specialist if allowable under state licensure laws) in the last 60 days.[^7]
5. Do not code conditions that have been resolved, do not affect the resident's current status, or do not drive the resident's plan of care during the 7-day look-back period; these are inactive diagnoses.[^8]
6. Check off each active disease; check all that apply.[^2]
7. Examples of diseases included for a disease category are not limiting; diseases to be coded in these categories are not meant to be limited to only those listed in the examples.[^9]
8. Listing a disease/diagnosis on the resident's medical record problem list is not sufficient for determining active or inactive status.[^10]
9. Monitoring of medications for adverse effects as usual nursing practice does not, for coding purposes, count as management or monitoring of the underlying disease.[^11]
10. Where practitioners have potentially misdiagnosed a resident with a condition for which there is a lack of appropriate diagnostic information in the medical record, the corresponding diagnosis in Section I should not be coded.[^12]
11. When Z codes are used for aftercare following a hospitalization, another diagnosis for the related primary medical condition should be checked in items I0100–I7900 or entered in I8000.[^13]
12. The primary medical condition coded in item I0020B must be included in items I0100–I8000, Active Diagnoses in the Last 7 Days.[^14]
13. If a resident has no active diagnoses from the listed items within the past 7 days, I7900 (none of the above active diagnoses) is the corresponding "None of Above" response for this section.[^15]

## What the chart must show

1. A physician-documented diagnosis (or by NP, PA, or CNS if allowable under state licensure laws) of the condition within the last 60 days; medical record sources include progress notes, the most recent history and physical, transfer documents, discharge summaries, diagnosis/problem list, and other resources as available. If a diagnosis/problem list is used, only diagnoses confirmed by the physician should be entered. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^16]
   Step 1 diagnosis identification (60-day window).
2. Diagnoses communicated verbally must be documented in the medical record by the physician to ensure follow-up. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^17]
3. Diagnostic information, including past history obtained from family members and close contacts, must be documented in the medical record by the physician to ensure validity and follow-up. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^18]
4. Check medical record information sources for the last 7 days to identify "active" diagnoses: transfer documents, physician progress notes, recent history and physical, recent discharge summaries, nursing assessments, nursing care plans, medication sheets, doctor's orders, consults and official diagnostic reports, and other sources as available. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^19]
   Step 2 active-status determination (7-day window).
5. Specific documentation in the medical record by a physician, nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or clinical nurse specialist that the condition is active (e.g., in progress notes, most recent history and physical, transfer notes, hospital discharge summary) is sufficient and requires no additional confirmation. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^20]
   Framed under "Coding Tips" as indicators that "may assist assessors".
6. In the absence of specific documentation that the disease is active: evidence of recent onset or acute exacerbation indicated by a positive study, test or procedure, hospitalization for acute symptoms, and/or recent change in therapy in the last 7 days (sources may include radiological reports, hospital discharge summaries, doctor's orders). *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^21]
7. In the absence of specific documentation that the disease is active: documented symptoms and abnormal signs indicating ongoing or decompensated disease in the last 7 days, with the symptom specifically attributed to the disease (sources may include radiological reports, nursing assessments and care plans, progress notes). *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^22]
   "Therefore, a symptom must be specifically attributed to the disease."
8. In the absence of specific documentation that the disease is active: documentation of ongoing therapy with medications or other interventions in the last 7 days to manage the condition, where the medication requires monitoring for therapeutic efficacy or for potentially severe side effects. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^23]
   Relevant for diabetes managed with insulin/oral agents plus glucose monitoring, but the pages give no diabetes-specific example.
9. Documentation beyond the problem list is required: to determine active status, the reviewer checks progress notes (including the history and physical) during the 7-day look-back period for treatment of symptoms, doctor's orders for medications, and documentation of physical or other therapy for functional limitations caused by the condition. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^24]
   Example uses arthritis; the principle is stated for the section generally.

## ⚠️ Conflicts — please read carefully

The manual (or two documents) say different things here. Both sides are shown; nothing has been dropped.

> **Two coexisting look-back windows for Section I active diagnosis items (including I2900): 60 days for diagnosis identification and 7 days for active status.**
>
> - *Federal manual:* Diagnosis identification (Step 1) uses a 60-day look-back period.[^25]
> - *Federal manual:* Diagnosis status (active or inactive, Step 2) uses a 7-day look-back period.[^1]
>
> Status: Resolved by the document itself: both windows apply in sequence — a diagnosis must be physician-documented within 60 days AND be active during the 7-day look-back period (p. I-9). lookback_days is recorded as 7, the observation window for active status.
