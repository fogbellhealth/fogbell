## Coding rules

1. Check all of the listed nutritional approaches that apply; if none apply, check K0520Z, None of the above.[^6]
2. Column 1 (On Admission): check all nutritional approaches performed during the first 3 days of the SNF PPS Stay.[^11]
3. Column 2 (While Not a Resident): check all nutritional approaches performed prior to admission/entry or reentry to the facility and within the 7-day look-back period; leave Column 2 blank if the resident was admitted/entered or reentered the facility more than 7 days ago.[^12]
4. When completing the Interim Payment Assessment (IPA), completion of items K0520A, K0520B, and K0520Z is required.[^13]
5. Column 3 (While a Resident): check all nutritional approaches performed after admission/entry or reentry to the facility and within the 7-day look-back period.[^1]
6. Column 4 (At Discharge): check all nutritional approaches performed within the last 3 days of the SNF PPS Stay.[^14]
7. K0520A includes any and all nutrition and hydration received by the nursing home resident during the observation period either at the nursing home, at the hospital as an outpatient or an inpatient, provided they were administered for nutrition or hydration.[^15]
8. Fluids that may be included in K0520A (when supporting documentation reflects the need for additional fluid intake specifically addressing a nutrition or hydration need): IV fluids or hyperalimentation including total parenteral nutrition (TPN) administered continuously or intermittently; IV fluids running at KVO (Keep Vein Open); IV fluids contained in IV Piggybacks; hypodermoclysis and subcutaneous ports in hydration therapy.[^16]
9. IV fluids can be coded in K0520A if needed to prevent dehydration when the additional fluid intake is specifically needed for nutrition and/or hydration; prevention of dehydration should be clinically indicated and supporting documentation should be provided in the medical record.[^17]
10. Do NOT code in K0520A: IV medications (code in O0110H); IV fluids used to reconstitute and/or dilute medications for IV administration; IV fluids administered as a routine part of an operative or diagnostic procedure or recovery room stay; IV fluids administered solely as flushes; parenteral/IV fluids administered in conjunction with chemotherapy or dialysis.[^18]
11. Enteral feeding formulas should not be coded as a mechanically altered diet, and should only be coded as K0520D, Therapeutic Diet when the enteral formula is altered to manage problematic health conditions (e.g., enteral formulas specific to residents with diabetes).[^19]
12. Only feeding tubes that are used to deliver nutritive substances and/or hydration during the assessment period are coded in K0520B.[^20]
13. Do not capture a trial of a mechanically altered diet (e.g., pureed food, thickened liquids) during the observation period in K0520C.[^21]
14. A mechanically altered diet should not automatically be considered a therapeutic diet.[^22]
15. Therapeutic diets are determined by why the diet is required, not by the content of what is provided or when it is served.[^23]
16. Nutritional supplements (whether given with, in-between, or instead of meals) are only coded in K0520D, Therapeutic Diet, when they are being administered as part of a therapeutic diet to manage problematic health conditions; a supplement alone does not constitute a therapeutic diet.[^24]
17. Food elimination diets related to food allergies (e.g., peanut allergy) can be coded as a therapeutic diet in K0520D.[^25]
18. K0710 (Percent Intake by Artificial Route) is completed only if Column 2 and/or Column 3 are checked for K0520A and/or K0520B.[^26]

## What the chart must show

1. Review the medical record to determine if any of the listed nutritional approaches were performed during the look-back period. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^27]
   Stated assessment step for the item.
2. For fluids coded in K0520A (parenteral/IV feeding), there must be supporting documentation that reflects the need for additional fluid intake specifically addressing a nutrition or hydration need; this documentation should be noted in the resident's medical record according to State and Federal Regulations and/or internal facility policy. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^28]
3. When IV fluids are coded to prevent dehydration, prevention of dehydration should be clinically indicated and supporting documentation should be provided in the medical record. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^29]
4. Absent documentation supporting a need for additional fluid intake, the IV fluid is not coded in K0520A (example: resident with adequate documented fluid intake receiving antibiotic in 100 cc normal saline — K0520A1 would NOT be checked). *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^30]

> **Multiple coexisting observation windows for K0520 (7-day look-back vs. 3-day SNF PPS Stay windows)**
>
> - *Federal manual:* Columns 2 and 3 use a 7-day look-back period (performed while not a resident / while a resident within the last 7 days).[^1]
> - *Federal manual:* Column 1 uses days 1 through 3 of the SNF PPS Stay and Column 4 uses the last 3 days of the SNF PPS Stay, not a 7-day window.[^14]
>
> Status: Resolved within the document: the windows are column-specific — Columns 2 and 3 use the 7-day look-back; Columns 1 and 4 use the first/last 3 days of the SNF PPS Stay.
