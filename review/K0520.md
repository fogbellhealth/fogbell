## Look-back window

**7 days**[^1]

Columns 2 and 3 use a 7-day look-back period (Column 2 = performed prior to admission/entry or reentry and within the last 7 days; Column 3 = performed after admission/entry or reentry and within the last 7 days). Column 1 uses a different window: the assessment period is days 1 through 3 of the SNF PPS Stay starting with A2400B. Column 4 uses the last 3 days of the SNF PPS Stay ending on A2400C. Under the general convention, the ARD is the last day of the observation period and counting is ARD + 6 previous calendar days for a 7-day window (rai-manual-v1.20.1, p. 2-21).

> **Note:** the manual states more than one time period for this item. See the *Conflicts* section below.

## Coding rules

1. Review the medical record to determine if any of the listed nutritional approaches were performed during the look-back period.[^11]
2. Check all that apply; if none apply, check K0520Z, None of the above.[^12]
3. Column 1: check all nutritional approaches performed during the first 3 days of the SNF PPS Stay.[^13]
4. Column 2: check all nutritional approaches performed prior to admission/entry or reentry to the facility and within the 7-day look-back period. Leave Column 2 blank if the resident was admitted/entered or reentered the facility more than 7 days ago.[^14]
5. When completing the Interim Payment Assessment (IPA), the completion of items K0520A, K0520B, and K0520Z is required.[^15]
6. Column 3: check all nutritional approaches performed after admission/entry or reentry to the facility and within the 7-day look-back period.[^1]
7. Column 4: check all nutritional approaches performed within the last 3 days of the SNF PPS Stay.[^16]
8. K0520A includes any and all nutrition and hydration received by the nursing home resident during the observation period either at the nursing home, at the hospital as an outpatient or an inpatient, provided they were administered for nutrition or hydration.[^17]
9. For K0520A, the following fluids may be included when there is supporting documentation reflecting the need for additional fluid intake specifically addressing a nutrition or hydration need: IV fluids or hyperalimentation including TPN administered continuously or intermittently; IV fluids running at KVO; IV fluids contained in IV Piggybacks; hypodermoclysis and subcutaneous ports in hydration therapy.[^18]
10. IV fluids can be coded in K0520A if needed to prevent dehydration if the additional fluid intake is specifically needed for nutrition and/or hydration; prevention of dehydration should be clinically indicated and supporting documentation should be provided in the medical record.[^19]
11. Do not code in K0520A: IV medications (code in O0110H); IV fluids used to reconstitute and/or dilute medications for IV administration; IV fluids administered as a routine part of an operative or diagnostic procedure or recovery room stay; IV fluids administered solely as flushes; parenteral/IV fluids administered in conjunction with chemotherapy or dialysis.[^20]
12. Enteral feeding formulas should not be coded as a mechanically altered diet, and should only be coded as K0520D, Therapeutic Diet, when the enteral formula is altered to manage problematic health conditions.[^21]
13. Only feeding tubes that are used to deliver nutritive substances and/or hydration during the assessment period are coded in K0520B.[^22]
14. Assessors should not capture a trial of a mechanically altered diet (e.g., pureed food, thickened liquids) during the observation period in K0520C.[^23]
15. Supplements (whether given with, in-between, or instead of meals) are only coded in K0520D, Therapeutic Diet, when they are being administered as part of a therapeutic diet to manage problematic health conditions; a nutritional supplement alone does not constitute a therapeutic diet.[^24]
16. Food elimination diets related to food allergies (e.g., peanut allergy) can be coded as a therapeutic diet.[^25]
17. A mechanically altered diet should not automatically be considered a therapeutic diet.[^26]

## ⚠️ Conflicts — please read carefully

The manual (or two documents) say different things here. Both sides are shown; nothing has been dropped.

> **Coexisting observation windows for K0520 columns**
>
> - *Federal manual:* Columns 2 and 3 use a 7-day look-back period.[^1]
> - *Federal manual:* Column 1 uses days 1 through 3 of the SNF PPS Stay and Column 4 uses the last 3 days of the SNF PPS Stay.[^7]
>
> Status: Not a contradiction: the document assigns different observation periods to different columns of the same item. lookback_days records the 7-day window used for Columns 2 and 3; the 3-day SNF PPS Stay windows for Columns 1 and 4 are recorded in lookback_notes.

> **Whether the K0520A look-back may extend into the preadmission period**
>
> - *Federal manual:* Item pages direct that Column 2 captures approaches performed prior to admission/entry or reentry (i.e., preadmission), and K0520A includes nutrition/hydration received at the hospital as an outpatient or inpatient.[^17]
> - *Federal manual:* General convention states the look-back period does not extend into the preadmission period unless item instructions state otherwise, naming Section K as an exception.[^30]
>
> Status: Resolved by the document itself: the general convention names Section K as an exception and defers to item instructions, so the K0520 Column 2 preadmission instructions govern.
