## Coding rules

1. Code E0500A (along with E0500B and E0500C) based on all of the behavioral symptoms coded in E0200.[^4]
2. Determine whether the behaviors coded in E0200 put the resident at significant risk of physical illness or injury.[^5]
3. Code 0, no, if none of the identified behavioral symptom(s) placed the resident at clinically significant risk for a physical illness or injury.[^2]
4. Code 1, yes, if any of the identified behavioral symptom(s) placed the resident at clinically significant risk for a physical illness or injury; an actual injury need not have occurred.[^3]
5. For E0500A, code based on whether the risk for physical injury or illness is known to occur commonly under similar circumstances (i.e., with residents who exhibit similar behavior in a similar environment). Physical injury is trauma that results in pain or other distressing physical symptoms, impaired organ function, physical disability, or other adverse consequences, regardless of the need for medical, surgical, nursing, or rehabilitative intervention.[^6]
6. E0500 is completed only when E0300 is coded 1, yes (i.e., any of E0200A, E0200B, or E0200C were coded 1, 2, or 3); if E0200A, E0200B, and E0200C are all coded 0, E0300 is coded 0 and the assessor skips to E0800, so E0500 is not completed.[^7]
7. When E0300 is coded 0 (no behavioral symptoms in E0200), skip to E0800, Rejection of Care—Presence & Frequency, rather than completing E0500.[^8]
8. Resident intent should not be taken into account when coding items in Section E.[^9]
