## Coding rules

1. Enter the number of Stage 1 pressure injuries that are currently present.[^1]
2. Enter 0 if no Stage 1 pressure injuries are currently present.[^2]
3. M0300A collects a single count data element, "Number of Stage 1 pressure injuries," under the Stage 1 definition printed on the item (intact skin with non-blanchable redness of a localized area usually over a bony prominence).[^3]
4. Code here only when the lesion assessed is primarily related to pressure and other conditions have been ruled out; if pressure is not the primary cause, do not code here.[^4]
5. A pressure injury with intact skin that is a deep tissue injury (DTI) must not be coded as a Stage 1 pressure injury; it is coded as unstageable.[^5]
6. For each pressure ulcer, determine the deepest anatomical stage; do not reverse or back stage; consider current and historical levels of tissue involvement.[^6]
7. If a stageable pressure ulcer has ever been classified at a higher numerical stage than what is observed now, it continues to be classified at the higher numerical stage until healed unless it becomes unstageable.[^7]
8. Reliance on only one descriptor is inadequate to determine staging between Stage 1 and deep tissue injury, because descriptors (temperature, tissue consistency) are similar for the two.[^8]
9. A previously closed pressure ulcer that opens again should be reported at its worst stage, unless currently presenting at a higher stage or unstageable.[^9]

## What the chart must show

1. Review the history of each pressure ulcer in the medical record when determining the stage to be coded. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^10]
   Stated as a required step in Step 1: Determine Deepest Anatomical Stage (M0300A–G).
2. Careful facility documentation and tracking of pressure ulcers supports accurate coding of this item. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^11]
3. Perform a head-to-toe/full body skin assessment focusing on bony prominences and pressure-bearing areas (sacrum, buttocks, heels, ankles, etc.) as the basis for identifying Stage 1 pressure injuries. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^12]
   Listed as Step 1 for Assessment for M0300A.
4. Check reddened areas for ability to blanch by firmly pressing a finger into the reddened tissue and then removing it; in non-blanchable areas there is no loss of skin color or pressure-induced pallor at the compressed site. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^13]
   Assessment technique step; supports the non-blanchable finding underlying the count.
5. Nursing homes can document healing using descriptive characteristics of the wound (depth, width, presence or absence of granulation tissue) or a validated pressure ulcer healing tool rather than reverse staging. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^14]

> **Observation window applicable to counting Stage 1 pressure injuries ("currently present" vs. the 7-day look-back used by the gateway item M0210)**
>
> - *Federal manual:* M0300A is coded on what is currently present, with no stated number of look-back days.[^1]
> - *Federal manual:* The gateway item M0210, which routes the assessor to M0300, is coded on presence of any pressure ulcer/injury (regardless of stage) in the past 7 days.[^15]
> - *Federal manual:* Staging for MDS assessment is described as coded in terms of what is assessed during the look-back period.[^16]
