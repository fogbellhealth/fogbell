## Look-back window

**7 days**[^1]

The D0150 pages do not state a numeric assessor observation/look-back window for this item; the 7-day value comes from the general convention (p. 3-3, standard MDS 3.0 look-back is 7 days unless otherwise stated), counted as the ARD plus the 6 previous calendar days (p. 2-21). The item's own pages state only that the interview is conducted during the look-back period of the ARD (p. D-2) and that if the resident interview was not conducted within the look-back period of the ARD, D0100 must be coded 1 and dashes entered in the resident interview items (p. D-3). Separately, the interview questions themselves use a 14-day (2-week) symptom-recall period ("Over the last 2 weeks, have you been bothered by any of the following problems?", p. D-3; frequency codes span 0–14 days, p. D-6); this is a symptom-recall period inside the interview questions, not the assessor observation window. The coexistence of the two windows is recorded in conflicting passages (none found).

> **Note:** the manual states more than one time period for this item. See the *Conflicts* section below.

## Coding rules

1. Conduct the Resident Mood Interview for any resident when D0100 = 1.[^11]
2. Attempt to conduct the interview with ALL residents.[^12]
3. Read each item as it is written and do not provide definitions; the item is scored based on the resident's own interpretation of the wording.[^13]
4. Each question must be asked in sequence to assess Symptom Presence (Column 1) and Symptom Frequency (Column 2) before proceeding to the next question.[^14]
5. Enter code 9 in Column 1 and leave Column 2 blank if the resident was unable or chose not to complete the assessment or responded nonsensically (unrelated, incomprehensible, incoherent, or not informative for the item being rated).[^15]
6. For a yes response, ask the resident how often they were bothered by the symptom over the last 2 weeks, using the D0150 Column 2 Symptom Frequency response choices and the cue card categories.[^16]
7. Gateway rule: if both D0150A1 and D0150B1 are coded 9, OR both D0150A2 and D0150B2 are coded 0 or 1, end the PHQ interview; otherwise continue with the remaining seven questions.[^17]
8. If both D0150A1 and D0150B1 are coded 9, leave D0150A2 and D0150B2 blank, end the PHQ-2©, leave D0160 Total Severity Score blank, and skip to D0700, Social Isolation.[^18]
9. If both D0150A2 and D0150B2 are coded 0 or 1, end the PHQ-2© and enter the total score from D0150A2 and D0150B2 in D0160, Total Severity Score.[^19]
10. For all other scenarios, proceed to ask the remaining seven questions (D0150C to D0150I of the PHQ-9©) and complete D0160, Total Severity Score.[^20]
11. Record the resident's responses as they are stated, regardless of whether the resident or the assessor attributes the symptom to something other than mood.[^21]
12. If Column 1 equals 0, enter 0 in Column 2.[^22]
13. If Column 1 equals 9 or dash, leave Column 2 blank.[^23]
14. Select only one frequency response per item.[^24]
15. If the resident has difficulty selecting between two frequency responses, code for the higher frequency.[^25]
16. For items containing more than one phrase (e.g., D0150F), if the resident gives different frequencies for different parts of a single item, select the highest frequency as the score for that item.[^26]
17. In the rare situation that the resident cannot provide a frequency following a yes response in Column 1, enter a dash in Column 2.[^27]
18. Residents may respond verbally, by pointing to their answers on the cue card, or by writing out their answers.[^28]
19. If the resident interview was not conducted within the look-back period of the ARD, item D0100 must be coded 1, Yes, and the standard "no information" code (a dash "-") entered in the resident interview items.[^29]
20. Resident refusal or unwillingness to participate in the interview results in D0100 being coded 1, Yes, and code 9, No response, being entered in Column 1, Symptom Presence; assessors then proceed to D0700, Social Isolation.[^30]
21. Conduct the interview in a private setting; if an interpreter is used, the interpreter should not attempt to determine the intent behind what is being translated, the outcome of the interview, or the meaning or significance of the resident's responses.[^31]

> **Which time window governs D0150: the ARD look-back period for conducting the interview vs. the 2-week symptom recall period inside the interview questions**
>
> - *Federal manual:* The Resident Mood Interview is conducted during the look-back period of the ARD (no number of days stated on the item's pages), and the standard MDS 3.0 look-back is 7 days unless otherwise stated.[^32]
> - *Federal manual:* The interview questions and all frequency codes reference symptoms over the last 2 weeks (up to 14 days), a longer recall period than the standard 7-day observation window.[^33]
>
> Status: unresolved — surface to user. Recorded as: assessor observation window = 7 days per general convention (p. 3-3); symptom-recall window inside the interview = last 2 weeks per the item's own pages.
