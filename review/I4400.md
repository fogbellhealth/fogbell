## Look-back window

**7 days**[^1]

The item is part of I0100–I8000, Active Diagnoses in the Last 7 Days. The assessor's observation window for determining whether the diagnosis is ACTIVE is 7 days (ARD plus the 6 previous calendar days per the general convention on p. 2-17/2-21). The manual states a second, separate window for this section: diagnosis identification (Step 1) requires a physician-documented diagnosis in the last 60 days (p. I-8). The 30-day exception stated on pp. I-9 and I-14 applies only to Item I2300 UTI, not to I4400.

> **Note:** the manual states more than one time period for this item. See the *Conflicts* section below.

## Coding rules

1. I4400 is the Neurological-category item for cerebral palsy within I0100–I8000, Active Diagnoses in the Last 7 Days.[^4]
2. Code diseases that have a documented diagnosis in the last 60 days and have a direct relationship to the resident's current functional status, cognitive status, mood or behavior status, medical treatments, nursing monitoring, or risk of death during the 7-day look-back period.[^5]
3. Do not code conditions that have been resolved, do not affect the resident's current status, or do not drive the resident's plan of care during the 7-day look-back period; these are inactive diagnoses.[^6]
4. Check off each active disease; check all that apply.[^2]
5. A resident with cerebral palsy with spastic quadriplegia is coded in I4400, Cerebral Palsy, and not in I5100, Quadriplegia; for severe physical disabilities with minimal purposeful movement, the primary physician-documented diagnosis is coded rather than the resulting paralysis or paresis.[^7]
6. Coding I5100 Quadriplegia is limited to spinal cord injuries and must be a primary diagnosis, not the result of another condition (such as cerebral palsy), which bears on whether the cerebral palsy item is the correct item to check.[^8]
7. If the primary medical condition is coded in item I0020B, it must also be included in items I0100–I8000, Active Diagnoses in the Last 7 Days.[^9]
8. Where a practitioner has potentially misdiagnosed a resident with a condition for which there is a lack of appropriate diagnostic information in the medical record, the corresponding Section I diagnosis should not be coded.[^10]
9. Diagnoses listed in parentheses in the item set are examples only and are not all-inclusive lists.[^11]

## What the chart must show

1. A physician-documented diagnosis (or by a nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or clinical nurse specialist if allowable under state licensure laws) of cerebral palsy in the last 60 days must be present in the medical record. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^12]
2. Acceptable medical record sources for the physician diagnosis include progress notes, the most recent history and physical, transfer documents, discharge summaries, and the diagnosis/problem list; if a diagnosis/problem list is used, only diagnoses confirmed by the physician should be entered. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^13]
3. Diagnoses communicated verbally must be documented in the medical record by the physician. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^14]
4. Diagnostic information, including past history obtained from family members and close contacts, must be documented in the medical record by the physician. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^15]
5. Evidence of active status must be sought in the medical record for the last 7 days: transfer documents, physician progress notes, recent history and physical, recent discharge summaries, nursing assessments, nursing care plans, medication sheets, doctor's orders, consults and official diagnostic reports. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^16]
6. Listing the diagnosis on the resident's problem list alone is not sufficient to establish active status; the reviewer must find treatment of symptoms, physician orders for medications, or documentation of therapy for functional limitations during the 7-day look-back period. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^17]
   Stated using arthritis as the example, but framed as a general rule for determining active/inactive status in this section.
7. Specific documentation by a physician, nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or clinical nurse specialist that the condition is active (found in progress notes, most recent history and physical, transfer notes, hospital discharge summary, etc.) is sufficient and requires no additional confirmation. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^18]
   Presented under "Coding Tips" as indicators that may assist assessors.
8. In the absence of specific documentation that the disease is active, active status may be confirmed by recent onset or acute exacerbation in the last 7 days; symptoms and abnormal signs indicating ongoing or decompensated disease in the last 7 days; or ongoing therapy with medications or other interventions requiring monitoring for therapeutic efficacy or severe side effects in the last 7 days. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^19]
   Indicator list continues on p. I-14 (ongoing therapy with medications requiring monitoring).
9. Routine nursing monitoring of medications for adverse effects does not by itself support active disease; that monitoring relates to management of pharmacotherapy, not management or monitoring of the underlying disease. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^20]

## ⚠️ Conflicts — please read carefully

The manual (or two documents) say different things here. Both sides are shown; nothing has been dropped.

> **Two coexisting look-back windows for this item: 60 days for diagnosis identification and 7 days for active status**
>
> - *Federal manual:* Diagnosis identification (Step 1) uses a 60-day look-back period; the physician-documented diagnosis must be in the last 60 days.[^21]
> - *Federal manual:* Diagnosis status (active or inactive, Step 2) uses a 7-day look-back period, and the section is titled "Active Diagnoses in the Last 7 Days."[^1]
>
> Status: Resolved by the document itself: the manual states both windows apply to different assessment steps — 60 days to identify the physician-documented diagnosis, 7 days to determine whether it is active. lookback_days is recorded as the 7-day active-status observation window.
