## Look-back window

**7 days**[^1]

Two look-back periods apply to this section. The assessor's active-status observation window is 7 days (I4400 is not one of the excepted items; only I2300 UTI uses a 30-day window). A separate 60-day window applies to Step 1, identification of a physician-documented diagnosis: "Diagnosis identification (Step 1) is a 60-day look-back period." (p. I-8). The manual does not state how the 7-day window is counted relative to the ARD on the supplied pages.

> **Note:** the manual states more than one time period for this item. See the *Conflicts* section below.

## Coding rules

1. I4400 is the Neurological-category checkbox for cerebral palsy within items I0100–I8000, Active Diagnoses in the Last 7 Days.[^4]
2. The item is a check-all-that-apply checkbox; check off each active disease.[^5]
3. The disease condition requires a physician-documented diagnosis (or by a nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or clinical nurse specialist if allowable under state licensure laws) in the last 60 days.[^6]
4. Once the diagnosis is identified, it must be determined to be active: it must have a direct relationship to the resident's current functional, cognitive, or mood or behavior status, medical treatments, nursing monitoring, or risk of death during the 7-day look-back period.[^7]
5. If a diagnosis/problem list is used as the source, only diagnoses confirmed by the physician should be entered.[^8]
6. Listing the disease/diagnosis on the resident's medical record problem list is not sufficient for determining active or inactive status.[^9]
7. A resident with cerebral palsy with spastic quadriplegia is coded in I4400 Cerebral Palsy, and not in I5100 Quadriplegia; for severe physical disability with minimal purposeful movement, the primary physician-documented diagnosis is coded rather than the resulting paralysis or paresis.[^10]
8. Where practitioners have potentially misdiagnosed a resident with a condition for which there is a lack of appropriate diagnostic information in the medical record, the corresponding Section I diagnosis should not be coded.[^11]
9. Examples given for a disease category are not all-inclusive; diseases to be coded in these categories are not limited to those listed.[^12]
10. If the primary medical condition is coded in I0020B, it must also be included in items I0100–I8000, Active Diagnoses in the Last 7 Days (relevant when cerebral palsy is the primary condition coded under I0020 code 07, Other Neurological Conditions).[^13]
11. If no listed diagnosis is active within the last 7 days, I7900 (None of the above active diagnoses) is used instead of checking disease items.[^14]

## What the chart must show

1. Physician (or NP/PA/CNS if allowable under state licensure laws) documentation of the cerebral palsy diagnosis within the last 60 days; medical record sources include progress notes, the most recent history and physical, transfer documents, discharge summaries, and the diagnosis/problem list. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^15]
   Step 1 requirement for identifying the diagnosis.
2. Diagnoses communicated verbally by the physician must be documented in the medical record by the physician. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^16]
3. Diagnostic information, including past history obtained from family members and close contacts, must be documented in the medical record by the physician. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^17]
4. Review medical record sources covering the last 7 days to identify 'active' status: transfer documents, physician progress notes, recent history and physical, recent discharge summaries, nursing assessments, nursing care plans, medication sheets, doctor's orders, consults and official diagnostic reports. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^18]
5. Specific documentation by a physician, nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or clinical nurse specialist that the condition is active is sufficient and requires no additional confirmation. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^19]
   Presented under "Coding Tips" as an indicator that may assist assessors.
6. In the absence of specific documentation that the disease is active, evidence of recent onset or acute exacerbation (positive study, test or procedure, hospitalization for acute symptoms, and/or recent change in therapy) in the last 7 days may confirm active disease; sources include radiological reports, hospital discharge summaries, doctor's orders. *(importance to an auditor: low)*[^20]
   Framed as an optional indicator.
7. Symptoms and abnormal signs indicating ongoing or decompensated disease in the last 7 days may confirm active disease, but a symptom must be specifically attributed to the disease; sources include radiological reports, nursing assessments and care plans, progress notes. *(importance to an auditor: low)*[^21]
   Framed as an optional indicator.
8. Ongoing therapy with medications or other interventions in the last 7 days to manage the condition, where the intervention requires monitoring for therapeutic efficacy or for potentially severe side effects, indicates active disease. *(importance to an auditor: low)*[^22]
   Framed as an optional indicator; general medication adverse-effect monitoring alone does not count (p. I-14).
9. Documentation must go beyond the problem list: reviewers should check progress notes (including the history and physical) during the 7-day look-back period for treatment of symptoms, doctor's orders for medications, and documentation of physical or other therapy for functional limitations caused by the condition. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^23]
   Stated for arthritis as an example, but framed as how to determine active status generally.

## ⚠️ Conflicts — please read carefully

The manual (or two documents) say different things here. Both sides are shown; nothing has been dropped.

> **Two coexisting look-back windows for Section I active diagnoses (60-day identification vs. 7-day active status)**
>
> - *Federal manual:* Diagnosis identification (Step 1) uses a 60-day look-back period.[^24]
> - *Federal manual:* Diagnosis status (active or inactive, Step 2) uses a 7-day look-back period.[^1]
>
> Status: Resolved by the document: both windows apply sequentially — the diagnosis must be documented within 60 days and must be active during the 7-day look-back period (p. I-9 restates both in the Coding Instructions). The assessor's observation window for active status is 7 days.
