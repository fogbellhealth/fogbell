## Coding rules

1. Stage 2 is defined on the item as: partial thickness loss of dermis presenting as a shallow open ulcer with a red or pink wound bed, without slough; may also present as an intact or open/ruptured blister.[^5]
2. M0300B1: enter the number of pressure ulcers currently present whose deepest anatomical stage is Stage 2.[^1]
3. If M0300B1 is 0, skip to M0300C, Stage 3.[^6]
4. M0300B2: enter the number of these Stage 2 pressure ulcers first noted at the time of admission/entry, and for residents reentering after a hospital stay, those acquired during the hospitalization (i.e., not acquired in the nursing facility prior to hospital admission); enter 0 if none.[^7]
5. Code here only if the lesion assessed is primarily related to pressure and other conditions have been ruled out; if pressure is not the primary cause, do not code here.[^8]
6. Stage 2 pressure ulcers by definition have partial thickness loss of the dermis; granulation tissue, slough, and eschar are not present in Stage 2 pressure ulcers.[^9]
7. Do not code skin tears, tape burns, moisture associated skin damage, or excoriation at M0300B.[^10]
8. When a pressure ulcer presents as an intact blister, examine the adjacent and surrounding area for signs of deep tissue injury; when a deep tissue injury is determined, do not code as a Stage 2.[^11]
9. If other conditions are ruled out and the tissue adjacent to or surrounding an intact blister demonstrates signs of tissue damage (e.g., color change, tenderness, bogginess or firmness, warmth or coolness), these characteristics suggest a deep tissue injury (DTI) rather than a Stage 2 pressure ulcer.[^12]
10. For each pressure ulcer, determine the deepest anatomical stage; at admission, code based on findings from the first skin assessment conducted on or after and as close to the admission as possible; do not reverse or back stage; consider current and historical levels of tissue involvement.[^13]
11. If a stageable pressure ulcer has ever been classified at a higher numerical stage than what is observed now, it should continue to be classified at the higher numerical stage until healed unless it becomes unstageable.[^14]
12. A previously closed pressure ulcer that opens again should be reported at its worst stage, unless currently presenting at a higher stage or unstageable.[^15]
13. "Present on admission" determination (referenced by M0300B step 5): for each pressure ulcer/injury, determine if it was present at the time of admission/entry or reentry and not acquired while the resident was in the care of the nursing home, considering current and historical levels of tissue involvement.[^16]
14. If the pressure ulcer/injury was present on admission/entry or reentry and subsequently increased in numerical stage during the resident's stay, the pressure ulcer is coded at that higher stage, and that higher stage should not be considered as "present on admission."[^17]
15. If a numerically stageable pressure ulcer/injury was present on admission/entry or reentry and becomes unstageable due to slough or eschar during the stay, it is coded at M0300F and should not be coded as "present on admission."[^18]
16. If a resident with a facility-acquired pressure ulcer/injury is hospitalized and returns with that ulcer/injury at the same numerical stage, it should not be coded as "present on admission."[^19]
17. If a resident with a pressure ulcer/injury that was "present on admission" is hospitalized and returns with that ulcer at the same numerical stage, it is still coded as "present on admission."[^20]
18. If a pressure ulcer/injury documented on admission closed and then reopens at the same stage (not a higher stage), the ulcer/injury is coded as "present on admission."[^21]
19. If two pressure ulcers that were both "present on admission" merge, the merged ulcer continues to be coded as "present on admission"; there must be an increase in numerical stage or a change to unstageable due to slough or eschar for it to be considered not "present on admission."[^22]

## What the chart must show

1. Perform a head-to-toe/full body skin assessment focusing on bony prominences and pressure-bearing areas (sacrum, buttocks, heels, ankles, etc.) as the basis for counting Stage 2 pressure ulcers. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^23]
   Listed as Step 1 of Steps for Assessment for M0300B.
2. Identify the number of these pressure ulcers that were present on admission/entry or reentry, following the instructions on page M-8. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^24]
   Supports the M0300B2 count.
3. Review the history of each pressure ulcer in the medical record; nursing homes that carefully document and track pressure ulcers will be able to more accurately code this item. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^25]
   From Step 1 (Determine Deepest Anatomical Stage) for M0300A–G.
4. Review the medical record for the history of the ulcer/injury when determining "present on admission." *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^26]
   Step 3, item 1, referenced by M0300B step 5.
5. Review for location and stage at the time of admission/entry or reentry. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^27]
   Step 3, item 2, referenced by M0300B step 5.
6. Nursing homes may document the healing of pressure ulcers using descriptive characteristics of the wound (depth, width, presence or absence of granulation tissue, etc.) or by using a validated pressure ulcer healing tool (reverse/back-staging is not used). *(importance to an auditor: low)*[^28]
   Framed as an option ("can document"), not a required record element.

> **Observation window applicable to counting Stage 2 pressure ulcers at M0300B**
>
> - *Federal manual:* M0300B coding instructions count pressure ulcers that are "currently present," with no stated number of look-back days.[^1]
> - *Federal manual:* The gateway item M0210, which routes the assessor to M0300, is coded on presence of any pressure ulcer/injury in the past 7 days (7-day look-back period).[^29]
> - *Federal manual:* Section M Planning-for-Care text states that initial numerical staging should be coded in terms of what is assessed during the look-back period.[^30]
