## Coding rules

1. Check N0415Z1, None of the above, if none of the medications in classes N0415A–N0415K were taken by the resident at any time during the observation period (or since admission/entry or reentry if less than 7 days).[^2]
2. N0415 is a "check all that apply" item; the None of the above row (Z) has only a Column 1 ("Is taking") check box and no Column 2 ("Indication noted") check box.[^4]
3. Column 1 of N0415 is checked if the resident is taking any medications by pharmacological classification during the 7-day observation period (or since admission/entry or reentry if less than 7 days); high-risk drug class medications are coded according to their pharmacological classification, not how they are being used.[^5]
4. A medication is coded in N0415 even if it was given only once during the look-back period (therefore a single dose of any listed class precludes coding None of the above).[^6]
5. Include any of these medications given to the resident by any route in any setting (e.g., at the nursing home, in a hospital emergency room) while a resident of the nursing home.[^7]
6. Long-acting medications given every few weeks or monthly are counted only if they are given during the 7-day look-back period (or since admission/entry or reentry if less than 7 days).[^8]
7. For transdermal patches, capture the medication only if the patch was applied to the resident's skin during the observation period.[^9]
8. Herbal and alternative medicine products should not be counted as medications (e.g., melatonin, chamomile, valerian root); over-the-counter sleeping medications are not coded as hypnotics.[^10]

## What the chart must show

1. Review the resident's medical record for documentation that any of these medications were received by the resident and for the indication of their use during the 7-day look-back period (or since admission/entry or reentry if less than 7 days). *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^11]
   Establishes the record basis for concluding that no listed high-risk class medication was received.
2. Review documentation from other health care settings where the resident may have received any of these medications while a resident of the nursing home (e.g., valium given in the emergency room). *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^12]
   Outside-setting administrations must be ruled out before checking None of the above.
3. Assessors should be able to identify the source(s) used to support coding the pharmacological classification on the MDS 3.0 (e.g., manufacturer's package insert, resident's pharmacist). *(importance to an auditor: low)*[^13]
   Framed as a suggestion ("may wish to") with an expectation that sources are identifiable.
