## Coding rules

1. Check N0415Z1 if none of the high-risk drug class medications listed in N0415 (A through K) were taken by the resident at any time during the observation period (or since admission/entry or reentry if less than 7 days).[^2]
2. Medications are coded in N0415 according to their pharmacological classification, not how they are being used; Column 1 is checked if the resident is taking any medications by pharmacological classification during the 7-day observation period (or since admission/entry or reentry if less than 7 days).[^4]
3. Code a medication even if it was given only once during the look-back period.[^5]
4. Include any of these medications given to the resident by any route in any setting (e.g., at the nursing home, in a hospital emergency room) while a resident of the nursing home.[^6]
5. Long-acting medications given every few weeks or monthly (e.g., fluphenazine decanoate, haloperidol decanoate) are counted only if they are given during the 7-day look-back period (or since admission/entry or reentry if less than 7 days).[^7]
6. For transdermal patches, only capture the medication if the patch was applied to the resident's skin during the observation period.[^8]
7. Over-the-counter sleeping medications are not coded as hypnotics, as they are not categorized as hypnotic medications.[^9]
8. Herbal and alternative medicine products (e.g., melatonin, chamomile, valerian root) are dietary supplements and should not be counted as medications.[^10]
9. Medications with more than one therapeutic category and/or pharmacological classification are coded in all categories/classifications assigned to the medication, regardless of how they are used.[^11]
10. Combination medications are coded in all categories/pharmacologic classes that constitute the combination.[^12]

## What the chart must show

1. Review the resident's medical record for documentation that any of these medications were received by the resident and for the indication of their use during the 7-day look-back period (or since admission/entry or reentry if less than 7 days). *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^13]
   Step 1 of Steps for Assessment for N0415; establishes the record review supporting a determination that no listed medications were taken.
2. Review documentation from other health care settings where the resident may have received any of these medications while a resident of the nursing home (e.g., valium given in the emergency room). *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^14]
   Step 2 of Steps for Assessment; relevant because outside-setting administrations would preclude checking Z1.
3. Assessors should be able to identify the source(s) used to support coding the MDS 3.0 pharmacological classification; facilities may wish to identify a consistently used resource. *(importance to an auditor: low)*[^15]
   Framed as a suggestion ("may wish"), though it states assessors should be able to identify the source(s).
4. Providers are responsible for coding each medication's pharmacological/therapeutic classification accurately; the listed reference resources are optional and not required for MDS completion. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^16]
