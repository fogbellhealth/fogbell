## Coding rules

1. E0800 records whether the resident rejected evaluation or care (e.g., bloodwork, taking medications, ADL assistance) that is necessary to achieve the resident's goals for health and well-being.[^5]
2. Do not include behaviors that have already been addressed (e.g., by discussion or care planning with the resident or family), and determined to be consistent with resident values, preferences, or goals.[^6]
3. Do not include behaviors that have already been addressed and determined to be consistent with the resident's values, preferences, or goals; residents who have made an informed choice about not wanting a particular treatment or procedure should not be identified as "rejecting care."[^7]
4. The intent of the item is to identify potential behavioral problems, not situations in which care has been rejected based on a choice consistent with the resident's preferences or goals for health and well-being, or a choice made on behalf of the resident by a family member or other proxy decision maker.[^8]
5. Once a rejection/decline of care is investigated and determined to be a matter of the resident's choice, education is provided and the resident's choices become part of the plan of care; on future assessments this behavior would not be coded in this item.[^9]
6. E0800 is reached from E0300 when E0200A, E0200B, and E0200C are all coded 0 (skip to Rejection of Care—Presence & Frequency item E0800).[^10]

## What the chart must show

1. Review the medical record (as part of the steps for assessment for this item). *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^11]
   Listed as step 1 of the Steps for Assessment.
2. Interview staff, across all shifts and disciplines, as well as others who had close interactions with the resident during the 7-day look-back period. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^12]
3. Review the record and consult staff to determine whether the rejected care is needed to achieve the resident's preferences and goals for health and well-being. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^13]
4. Review the medical record to find out whether the care rejection behavior was previously addressed and documented in discussions or in care planning with the resident, family, or significant other and determined to be an informed choice consistent with the resident's values, preferences, or goals; or whether the behavior represents an objection to the way care is provided, but acceptable alternative care and/or approaches to care have been identified and employed. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^14]
   Key documentation criterion distinguishing codeable rejection of care from an addressed, informed choice.
5. If the resident exhibits behavior that appears to communicate a rejection of care (and that rejection behavior has not been previously determined to be consistent with the resident's values or goals), ask them directly whether the behavior is meant to decline or refuse care. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^15]
6. If the resident is unable or unwilling to respond to questions about their rejection of care or goals for health care and well-being, interview the family or significant other to ascertain the resident's health care preferences and goals. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^16]
