## Coding rules

1. E0800 asks whether the resident rejected evaluation or care (e.g., bloodwork, taking medications, ADL assistance) that is necessary to achieve the resident's goals for health and well-being; behaviors already addressed (e.g., by discussion or care planning with the resident or family) and determined to be consistent with resident values, preferences, or goals are not included.[^6]
2. Do not include behaviors that have already been addressed and determined to be consistent with the resident's values, preferences, or goals; residents who have made an informed choice about not wanting a particular treatment or procedure should not be identified as "rejecting care."[^7]
3. Frequency coding disregards the number of episodes occurring on any one day; only the number of days on which the behavior occurred determines the code.[^8]
4. Once rejection/decline of care is investigated and determined to be a matter of resident choice, education is provided and the resident's choices become part of the plan of care; on future assessments this behavior would not be coded in this item.[^9]
5. E0800 is the skip target from E0300: if E0200A, E0200B, and E0200C are all coded 0, skip to Rejection of Care—Presence & Frequency item (E0800).[^10]
6. Care rejection may be manifested by verbally declining, statements of refusal, or through physical behaviors that convey aversion to, result in avoidance of, or interfere with the receipt of care.[^11]

## What the chart must show

1. Review the medical record. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^12]
   Step 1 of Steps for Assessment.
2. Interview staff, across all shifts and disciplines, as well as others who had close interactions with the resident during the 7-day look-back period. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^1]
3. Review the record and consult staff to determine whether the rejected care is needed to achieve the resident's preferences and goals for health and well-being. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^13]
4. Review the medical record to find out whether the care rejection behavior was previously addressed and documented in discussions or in care planning with the resident, family, or significant other and determined to be an informed choice consistent with the resident's values, preferences, or goals; or whether the behavior represents an objection to the way care is provided, but acceptable alternative care and/or approaches to care have been identified and employed. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^14]
   Documentation of prior discussion/care planning is the basis for excluding the behavior from coding.
5. If the resident exhibits behavior that appears to communicate a rejection of care (and that rejection behavior has not been previously determined to be consistent with the resident's values or goals), ask them directly whether the behavior is meant to decline or refuse care. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^15]
6. If the resident is unable or unwilling to respond to questions about their rejection of care or goals for health care and well-being, interview the family or significant other to ascertain the resident's health care preferences and goals. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^16]
