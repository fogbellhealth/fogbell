## Look-back window

**Not stated in the manual for this item.**

The M0300C section itself does not state an observation/look-back window; it instructs the assessor to code ulcers "currently present." A 7-day look-back is stated for the gateway item M0210 ("Code based on the presence of any pressure ulcer/injury (regardless of stage) in the past 7 days.", p. M-5), but the manual does not explicitly assign that window to M0300C, so the look-back window is left Not found.

> **Note:** the manual states more than one time period for this item. See the *Conflicts* section below.

## Coding rules

1. Enter the number of pressure ulcers currently present whose deepest anatomical stage is Stage 3 in M0300C1.[^1]
2. Enter 0 in M0300C1 if no Stage 3 pressure ulcers are present and skip to M0300D, Stage 4.[^4]
3. In M0300C2, enter the number of these Stage 3 pressure ulcers first noted at Stage 3 at the time of admission/entry, and for residents reentering after a hospital stay, those Stage 3 ulcers acquired during the hospitalization (i.e., not acquired in the nursing facility prior to hospital admission).[^5]
4. Enter 0 in M0300C2 if no Stage 3 pressure ulcers were first noted at the time of admission/entry or reentry.[^6]
5. For coding purposes, determine that the lesion assessed is primarily related to pressure and other conditions have been ruled out; if pressure is not the primary cause, do not code here.[^7]
6. Do not code moisture-associated skin damage or excoriation as a Stage 3 pressure ulcer.[^8]
7. Bone/tendon/muscle is not visible or directly palpable in a Stage 3 pressure ulcer (distinguishing it from Stage 4).[^9]
8. Stage 3 depth varies by anatomical location: ulcers can be shallow on areas lacking subcutaneous tissue (bridge of nose, ear, occiput, malleolus), and extremely deep in areas of significant adiposity.[^10]
9. Determine the deepest anatomical stage for each pressure ulcer; at admission code based on the first skin assessment conducted on or after and as close to admission as possible; do not reverse or back stage; consider current and historical levels of tissue involvement.[^11]
10. If a stageable pressure ulcer has ever been classified at a higher numerical stage than what is observed now, it should continue to be classified at the higher numerical stage until healed unless it becomes unstageable.[^12]
11. A previously closed pressure ulcer that opens again should be reported at its worst stage, unless currently presenting at a higher stage or unstageable.[^13]
12. If a pressure ulcer was present on admission/entry or reentry and subsequently increased in numerical stage during the stay, code it at the higher stage and do not consider that higher stage "present on admission."[^14]
13. If a pressure ulcer/injury was unstageable on admission/entry or reentry and later becomes numerically stageable, it is considered "present on admission" at the stage at which it first becomes numerically stageable; a subsequent increase in numerical stage is not "present on admission."[^15]
14. If a resident with a facility-acquired pressure ulcer is hospitalized and returns with that ulcer at the same numerical stage, it should not be coded as "present on admission."[^16]
15. If a pressure ulcer/injury increases in numerical stage during hospitalization, it should be coded as "present on admission" upon reentry.[^17]
16. If a pressure ulcer was numerically staged, became unstageable, and is subsequently debrided sufficiently to be numerically staged, compare its numerical stage before and after; if the stage increased, code as not "present on admission."[^18]
17. Once a pressure ulcer is debrided of slough and/or eschar such that anatomic depth can be determined, code the ulcer for the reclassified stage; complete debridement is not required for reclassification.[^19]
18. Once a deep tissue injury has opened to an ulcer, reclassify the ulcer into the appropriate stage and code it at the reclassified stage.[^20]
19. If a resident had a pressure ulcer/injury that healed during the look-back period of the current assessment, do not code the ulcer/injury on the assessment.[^21]
20. If two pressure ulcers/injuries occur on the same bony prominence and are separated, at least superficially, by skin, count them as two separate pressure ulcers/injuries and stage and measure each separately.[^22]
21. If a pressure ulcer is surgically closed with a flap or graft, code it as a surgical wound and not as a pressure ulcer; if the flap or graft fails, continue to code as a surgical wound until healed.[^23]
22. M0300C is completed only when M0210 is coded 1 (resident has one or more unhealed pressure ulcers/injuries); if M0210 = 0, skip to M1030.[^24]

## What the chart must show

1. Review the medical record for the history of each pressure ulcer, including location and stage at the time of admission/entry or reentry, to determine "present on admission" status. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^25]
   Referenced by M0300C Steps for Assessment item 4 (identify number present on admission/entry or reentry).
2. Review the history of each pressure ulcer in the medical record; nursing homes that carefully document and track pressure ulcers will be able to more accurately code this item. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^26]
3. Perform a head-to-toe/full body skin assessment focusing on bony prominences and pressure-bearing areas to identify all Stage 3 pressure ulcers currently present. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^27]
   Stated as an assessment step, not explicitly as a documentation requirement.
4. Nursing homes can document healing of pressure ulcers using descriptive wound characteristics (depth, width, presence or absence of granulation tissue) or a validated pressure ulcer healing tool, rather than reverse staging. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^28]
