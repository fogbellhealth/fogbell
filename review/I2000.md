## Look-back window

**7 days**[^1]

Two look-back periods apply to this section. The assessor's observation window for determining whether the diagnosis is ACTIVE is 7 days (p. I-8); counted per the general convention as ARD + 6 previous calendar days (rai-manual-v1.20.1, p. 2-17: "ARD + 6 previous calendar days"). A separate 60-day window applies to Step 1, diagnosis identification: the disease must have a physician-documented diagnosis in the last 60 days (p. I-8, p. I-9). The 30-day exception stated for the section applies only to Item I2300 UTI, not to I2000.

> **Note:** the manual states more than one time period for this item. See the *Conflicts* section below.

## Coding rules

1. I2000 Pneumonia is one of the checkbox diagnoses in the Infections category of Active Diagnoses in the Last 7 Days; check all that apply.[^4]
2. Code the disease only if it has a documented diagnosis in the last 60 days AND has a direct relationship to the resident's current functional status, cognitive status, mood or behavior status, medical treatments, nursing monitoring, or risk of death during the 7-day look-back period.[^2]
3. The diagnosis must be physician-documented (or documented by a nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or clinical nurse specialist if allowable under state licensure laws) in the last 60 days.[^5]
4. Do not check the item for conditions that have been resolved, do not affect the resident's current status, or do not drive the resident's plan of care during the 7-day look-back period; these are inactive diagnoses.[^6]
5. Check off each active disease; check all that apply.[^7]
6. If none of the listed diagnoses (including pneumonia) are active within the past 7 days, code I7900, None of the above active diagnoses.[^8]
7. Specific physician documentation that the condition is active is sufficient to code it active and requires no additional confirmation.[^9]
8. In the absence of specific documentation that the disease is active, recent onset or acute exacerbation indicated by a positive study, test or procedure, hospitalization for acute symptoms, and/or recent change in therapy in the last 7 days may confirm active disease (e.g., new diagnosis of pneumonia indicated by chest X-ray).[^10]
9. Symptoms and abnormal signs indicating ongoing or decompensated disease in the last 7 days may confirm active disease, but a symptom must be specifically attributed to the disease by a physician (e.g., a productive cough would confirm a diagnosis of pneumonia if specifically noted as such by a physician).[^11]
10. Listing the diagnosis on the resident's medical record problem list is not sufficient for determining active or inactive status.[^12]
11. Ongoing therapy with medications or other interventions in the last 7 days to manage the condition, requiring monitoring for therapeutic efficacy or for potentially severe side effects, indicates active disease.[^13]
12. Routine nursing monitoring of medications for adverse effects does not by itself establish active disease; for coding purposes that monitoring relates to management of pharmacotherapy, not management or monitoring of the underlying disease.[^14]
13. If a disease or condition is not specifically listed among the checkbox items, enter the diagnosis and ICD code in item I8000, Additional active diagnosis.[^15]
14. If the resident is receiving aftercare following hospitalization, a Z code may be assigned; when Z codes are used, another diagnosis for the related primary medical condition should be checked in items I0100–I7900 or entered in I8000.[^16]
15. If pneumonia is the primary medical condition coded in I0020B, it must also be included in items I0100–I8000, Active Diagnoses in the Last 7 Days.[^17]

## What the chart must show

1. Physician-documented diagnosis of pneumonia (or by NP, PA, or clinical nurse specialist if allowable under state licensure laws) within the last 60 days, found in the medical record. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^5]
   Step 1 of the two-step assessment process.
2. Acceptable medical record sources for the physician diagnosis: progress notes, the most recent history and physical, transfer documents, discharge summaries, diagnosis/problem list, and other resources as available; if a diagnosis/problem list is used, only diagnoses confirmed by the physician should be entered. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^18]
3. Diagnoses communicated verbally must be documented in the medical record by the physician to ensure follow-up. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^19]
4. Diagnostic information, including past history obtained from family members and close contacts, must be documented in the medical record by the physician to ensure validity and follow-up. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^20]
5. Evidence in the last 7 days from these medical record sources supporting active status: transfer documents, physician progress notes, recent history and physical, recent discharge summaries, nursing assessments, nursing care plans, medication sheets, doctor's orders, consults and official diagnostic reports. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^21]
6. Radiological report (e.g., chest X-ray) documenting new onset of pneumonia may be used as a source to confirm active disease in the absence of specific documentation of activity. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^22]
   Framed as an indicator that may be used, not as required documentation.
7. Physician notation specifically attributing a symptom (e.g., productive cough) to pneumonia, in radiological reports, nursing assessments and care plans, or progress notes. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^23]

## ⚠️ Conflicts — please read carefully

The manual (or two documents) say different things here. Both sides are shown; nothing has been dropped.

> **Coexisting look-back windows for Section I active diagnoses (60-day identification vs. 7-day active status)**
>
> - *Federal manual:* Diagnosis identification (Step 1) uses a 60-day look-back period.[^24]
> - *Federal manual:* Diagnosis status (active or inactive, Step 2) uses a 7-day look-back period.[^1]
>
> Status: Resolved by the document itself: the two windows are complementary steps, not competing rules — the diagnosis must be documented within 60 days and must be active during the 7-day look-back period (p. I-9). lookback_days is recorded as the 7-day observation window for active status.
