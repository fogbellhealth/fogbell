## Look-back window

**7 days**[^1]

Two look-back periods are stated for this section and both apply to I4900: diagnosis identification (Step 1) uses a 60-day look-back ("physician-documented diagnosis ... in the last 60 days", p. I-8), and diagnosis status active/inactive (Step 2) uses a 7-day look-back (p. I-8). The assessor's observation window for determining whether the diagnosis is active is 7 days, so the look-back window = 7. Per the general convention, the ARD is day 1 of the look-back period and a 7-day window is the ARD plus the 6 previous calendar days (rai-manual-v1.20.1, p. 2-21); the item's own pages do not restate how the days are counted. The 30-day exception stated on pp. I-9/I-14 applies only to Item I2300 (UTI), not to I4900.

> **Note:** the manual states more than one time period for this item. See the *Conflicts* section below.

## Coding rules

1. I4900 captures hemiplegia or hemiparesis, listed under the Neurological disease category of Active Diagnoses in the Last 7 Days.[^4]
2. Code diseases that have a documented diagnosis in the last 60 days and have a direct relationship to the resident's current functional status, cognitive status, mood or behavior status, medical treatments, nursing monitoring, or risk of death during the 7-day look-back period.[^2]
3. The disease conditions in this section require a physician-documented diagnosis (or by a nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or clinical nurse specialist if allowable under state licensure laws) in the last 60 days.[^5]
4. Do not include conditions that have been resolved, do not affect the resident's current status, or do not drive the resident's plan of care during the 7-day look-back period; these are inactive diagnoses.[^6]
5. Check off each active disease; check all that apply.[^7]
6. Examples of diseases included for a disease category are not exhaustive; diseases coded in these categories are not limited to only those listed in the examples.[^8]
7. If a disease or condition is not specifically listed, enter the diagnosis and ICD code in item I8000, Additional active diagnosis.[^9]
8. If no listed condition is an active diagnosis, code I7900, none of the above active diagnoses within the past 7 days.[^10]
9. For individuals with severe physical disabilities where there is minimal ability for purposeful movement, the primary physician-documented diagnosis should be coded on the MDS and not the resulting paralysis or paresis from that condition (stated in the coding tip for Item I5100 Quadriplegia; e.g., cerebral palsy with spastic quadriplegia is coded in I4400, not I5100).[^11]
10. If the primary medical condition category coded in I0020B applies, it must also be included in items I0100–I8000: Active Diagnoses in the Last 7 Days.[^12]
11. Where a practitioner has potentially misdiagnosed a resident with a condition for which there is a lack of appropriate diagnostic information in the medical record, the corresponding diagnosis in Section I should not be coded.[^13]

## What the chart must show

1. A physician-documented diagnosis (or by a nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or clinical nurse specialist if allowable under state licensure laws) of hemiplegia or hemiparesis within the last 60 days must exist in the medical record. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^5]
   Step 1 of the two-step assessment process.
2. Acceptable medical record sources for physician diagnoses: progress notes, most recent history and physical, transfer documents, discharge summaries, diagnosis/problem list, and other resources as available; if a diagnosis/problem list is used, only diagnoses confirmed by the physician should be entered. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^14]
3. Diagnoses communicated verbally must be documented in the medical record by the physician to ensure follow-up. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^15]
4. Diagnostic information, including past history obtained from family members and close contacts, must also be documented in the medical record by the physician to ensure validity and follow-up. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^16]
5. Check these medical record sources for the last 7 days to identify "active" diagnoses: transfer documents, physician progress notes, recent history and physical, recent discharge summaries, nursing assessments, nursing care plans, medication sheets, doctor's orders, consults and official diagnostic reports, and other sources as available. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^17]
6. Specific physician (or NP/PA/CNS) documentation that the condition is active is sufficient documentation of active disease and requires no additional confirmation; such documentation may be found in progress notes, most recent history and physical, transfer notes, hospital discharge summary, etc. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^18]
   Framed as a Coding Tip indicator.
7. In the absence of specific documentation that the disease is active, active disease may be confirmed by recent onset or acute exacerbation indicated by a positive study, test or procedure, hospitalization for acute symptoms, and/or recent change in therapy in the last 7 days (sources may include radiological reports, hospital discharge summaries, doctor's orders). *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^19]
   Listed as an indicator that "may be used" to confirm active disease.
8. Symptoms and abnormal signs indicating ongoing or decompensated disease in the last 7 days may confirm active disease; a symptom must be specifically attributed to the disease (sources may include radiological reports, nursing assessments and care plans, progress notes). *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^20]
9. Listing a disease/diagnosis on the resident's medical record problem list is not sufficient for determining active or inactive status; the reviewer would check progress notes (including the history and physical) during the 7-day look-back period for treatment of symptoms, doctor's orders for medications, and documentation of physical or other therapy for functional limitations caused by the condition. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^21]
10. Ongoing therapy with medications or other interventions to manage a condition that requires monitoring for therapeutic efficacy or for potentially severe side effects in the last 7 days indicates active disease; monitoring of medications for adverse effects alone relates to management of pharmacotherapy, not to management or monitoring of the underlying disease. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^22]
   Second bullet on p. I-14 clarifies that routine adverse-effect monitoring does not by itself establish active disease.

## ⚠️ Conflicts — please read carefully

The manual (or two documents) say different things here. Both sides are shown; nothing has been dropped.

> **Coexistence of a 60-day and a 7-day look-back window for this section**
>
> - *Federal manual:* Diagnosis identification (Step 1) uses a 60-day look-back period.[^23]
> - *Federal manual:* Diagnosis status: active or inactive (Step 2) uses a 7-day look-back period.[^1]
>
> Status: Resolved by the document itself: it explicitly states "There are two look-back periods for this section" — 60 days for identifying a physician-documented diagnosis and 7 days for determining that the diagnosis is active (p. I-8, reiterated in the Coding Instructions on p. I-9).
