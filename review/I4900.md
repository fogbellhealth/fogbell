## Look-back window

**7 days**[^1]

The section I0100–I8000 (which contains I4900) has two stated look-back periods: diagnosis identification (Step 1) is a 60-day look-back for the physician-documented diagnosis, and diagnosis status active/inactive (Step 2) is a 7-day look-back. The assessor's observation window against which 'active' status is judged is 7 days; the 60-day window applies to identifying that a physician diagnosis exists. The 30-day exception stated in the manual applies only to Item I2300 UTI, not to I4900. The manual does not state how the days are counted relative to the ARD on these pages.

> **Note:** the manual states more than one time period for this item. See the *Conflicts* section below.

## Coding rules

1. I4900 is the Neurological-category active diagnosis item for hemiplegia or hemiparesis.[^4]
2. I4900 appears in the Active Diagnoses in the last 7 days item set, under the Neurological category, and is coded by checkbox (check all that apply).[^5]
3. Code the disease only if it has a documented diagnosis in the last 60 days AND has a direct relationship to the resident's current functional status, cognitive status, mood or behavior status, medical treatments, nursing monitoring, or risk of death during the 7-day look-back period.[^6]
4. The disease condition requires a physician-documented diagnosis (or by a nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or clinical nurse specialist if allowable under state licensure laws) in the last 60 days.[^7]
5. Do not code conditions that have been resolved, do not affect the resident's current status, or do not drive the plan of care during the 7-day look-back period; these are inactive diagnoses.[^3]
6. Diseases to be coded in a category are not limited to the listed examples; the parenthetical examples are not all-inclusive lists.[^8]
7. If the primary medical condition coded in I0020B is hemiplegia/hemiparesis-related, that primary medical condition must also be included in items I0100–I8000: Active Diagnoses in the Last 7 Days.[^9]
8. When a Z code is assigned for aftercare following hospitalization, another diagnosis for the related primary medical condition should be checked in items I0100–I7900 or entered in I8000.[^10]
9. If none of the listed active diagnoses (including I4900) apply within the last 7 days, code I7900, None of the above active diagnoses.[^11]
10. Where a practitioner has potentially misdiagnosed a resident with a condition for which there is a lack of appropriate diagnostic information in the medical record, the corresponding Section I diagnosis should not be coded.[^12]
11. Stated in the manual's coding tips for Item I5100 Quadriplegia (and therefore relevant when paresis/paralysis results from another condition): for individuals with severe physical disabilities where there is minimal ability for purposeful movement, the primary physician-documented diagnosis should be coded on the MDS and not the resulting paralysis or paresis from that condition.[^13]

## What the chart must show

1. Physician (or NP/PA/CNS if allowable under state licensure laws) documentation of the diagnosis within the last 60 days; medical record sources include progress notes, the most recent history and physical, transfer documents, discharge summaries, and the diagnosis/problem list (only diagnoses confirmed by the physician may be used from a problem list). *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^14]
2. Diagnoses communicated verbally must be documented in the medical record by the physician. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^15]
3. Diagnostic information, including past history obtained from family members and close contacts, must be documented in the medical record by the physician. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^16]
4. Evidence of active status must be sought in the last 7 days from: transfer documents, physician progress notes, recent history and physical, recent discharge summaries, nursing assessments, nursing care plans, medication sheets, doctor's orders, consults and official diagnostic reports. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^17]
5. Specific documentation by a physician, nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or clinical nurse specialist that the condition is active is sufficient and requires no additional confirmation. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^18]
   Framed as an indicator that 'may assist assessors' in determining active status.
6. In the absence of specific documentation that the disease is active, active status may be confirmed by recent onset or acute exacerbation indicated by a positive study, test or procedure, hospitalization for acute symptoms, and/or recent change in therapy in the last 7 days. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^19]
7. Active status may also be confirmed by symptoms and abnormal signs indicating ongoing or decompensated disease in the last 7 days; a symptom must be specifically attributed to the disease. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^20]
8. Listing the disease/diagnosis on the resident's problem list is not sufficient to determine active or inactive status; the reviewer checks progress notes during the 7-day look-back for treatment of symptoms, doctor's orders for medications, and documentation of physical or other therapy for functional limitations caused by the condition. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^21]
9. Ongoing therapy with medications or other interventions to manage the condition that requires monitoring for therapeutic efficacy or for potentially severe side effects in the last 7 days supports active status; monitoring of medications for adverse effects alone (pharmacotherapy management) does not. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^22]
   Paired with the statement on the same page that nurses' monitoring of all medications for adverse effects relates to pharmacotherapy management, not to management/monitoring of the underlying disease.

## ⚠️ Conflicts — please read carefully

The manual (or two documents) say different things here. Both sides are shown; nothing has been dropped.

> **Two look-back windows apply to the I0100–I8000 active diagnosis items, including I4900**
>
> - *Federal manual:* Diagnosis identification (Step 1) uses a 60-day look-back period for the physician-documented diagnosis.[^23]
> - *Federal manual:* Diagnosis status (active or inactive, Step 2) uses a 7-day look-back period.[^1]
>
> Status: Resolved by the document itself: the manual states both windows apply in sequence — a diagnosis documented in the last 60 days must also be active during the 7-day look-back period (p. I-9). The assessor's observation window recorded in lookback_days is 7 days.
