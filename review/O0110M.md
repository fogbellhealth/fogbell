## Coding rules

1. Code O0110M only when the resident requires transmission-based precautions AND single room isolation (alone in a separate room) because of active infection (symptomatic and/or positive test and in the contagious stage) with highly transmissible or epidemiologically significant pathogens acquired by physical contact or airborne or droplet transmission.[^4]
2. Do not code this item if the resident only has a history of infectious disease (e.g., s/p MRSA or s/p C-Diff with no active symptoms).[^5]
3. Do not code this item if the precautions are standard precautions (hand hygiene compliance, glove use, and possibly masks, eye protection, and gowns), because these apply to everyone.[^6]
4. Code for "single room isolation" only when all four conditions are met: (1) active infection with highly transmissible or epidemiologically significant pathogens acquired by physical contact or airborne or droplet transmission; (2) precautions are over and above standard precautions, i.e., transmission-based (contact, droplet, and/or airborne) precautions in effect; (3) the resident is in a room alone because of active infection and cannot have a roommate, not cohorted regardless of whether the roommate has a similar active infection; (4) the resident must remain in their room, with all services brought to the resident.[^7]
5. Examples of when the isolation criterion would not apply include urinary tract infections, encapsulated pneumonia, and wound infections.[^8]
6. If a facility transports a resident who meets the criteria for single room isolation to another healthcare setting for medically needed services the facility cannot provide (e.g., dialysis, chemotherapy, blood transfusions), the facility may still code O0110M for single room isolation since isolation is still maintained while the resident is in the facility.[^9]
7. Facilities may code treatments, programs and procedures that the resident performed themselves independently or after set-up by facility staff; do not code services provided solely in conjunction with a surgical or diagnostic procedure (surgical procedures include routine pre- and post-operative procedures).[^10]
8. Review the resident's medical record to determine whether or not the resident received or performed any of the treatments, procedures, or programs within the assessment period defined for each column.[^11]
9. When coding for isolation, the facility should review the resident's status and determine if the criteria for a Significant Change of Status Assessment (SCSA) is met based on the effect the infection has on the resident's function and plan of care; regardless of whether SCSA criteria are met, a modification of the plan of care will likely need to be completed.[^12]

## What the chart must show

1. Medical record evidence, reviewed for the assessment period, that the treatment/procedure/program was received or performed by the resident. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^11]
   The only documentation-review step stated for O0110; the manual does not list item-specific documentation requirements for O0110M beyond the four coding conditions.
2. Evidence that transmission-based precautions (contact, droplet, and/or airborne) were in effect, over and above standard precautions. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^13]
   Stated as a mandatory condition for coding.
3. Evidence that the resident was in a room alone because of active infection and could not have a roommate (not cohorted with another infected resident). *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^14]
4. Evidence that the resident remained in their room, with all services (e.g., rehabilitation, activities, dining) brought to the resident. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^15]
5. Evidence of active infection (symptomatic and/or positive test, in the contagious stage) with highly transmissible or epidemiologically significant pathogens acquired by contact, airborne, or droplet transmission. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^16]
