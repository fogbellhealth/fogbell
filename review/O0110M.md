## Coding rules

1. Check the item only if the treatment/program was received or performed after admission/entry or reentry to the facility and within the last 14 days (column b, While a Resident).[^1]
2. On the item set, O0110M1 (Isolation or quarantine for active infectious disease) offers only the "While a Resident" (column b) checkbox; standard body/fluid precautions are excluded from this item.[^5]
3. Code only when the resident requires transmission-based precautions AND single room isolation (alone in a separate room) because of active infection (symptomatic and/or positive test and in the contagious stage) with highly transmissible or epidemiologically significant pathogens acquired by physical contact or airborne or droplet transmission.[^2]
4. Do not code this item if the resident only has a history of infectious disease with no active symptoms (e.g., s/p MRSA or s/p C-Diff).[^6]
5. Do not code this item if the precautions in place are standard precautions (hand hygiene, glove use, and possibly masks, eye protection, gowns), because these apply to everyone.[^7]
6. Examples of when the isolation criterion would not apply include urinary tract infections, encapsulated pneumonia, and wound infections.[^8]
7. Condition 1 of 4 for coding "single room isolation": the resident has active infection with highly transmissible or epidemiologically significant pathogens acquired by physical contact or airborne or droplet transmission.[^9]
8. Condition 2 of 4: precautions must be over and above standard precautions — transmission-based precautions (contact, droplet, and/or airborne) must be in effect.[^10]
9. Condition 3 of 4: the resident is in a room alone because of active infection and cannot have a roommate; the resident must not be cohorted with a roommate even if the roommate has a similar active infection requiring isolation.[^11]
10. Condition 4 of 4: the resident must remain in their room, with all services brought to the resident (e.g., rehabilitation, activities, dining).[^12]
11. If a facility transports a resident who meets the single room isolation criteria to another healthcare setting for medically needed services the facility cannot provide (e.g., dialysis, chemotherapy, blood transfusions), O0110M may still be coded for single room isolation because isolation is still maintained while the resident is in the facility (CDC transport guidelines should be followed).[^13]
12. General O0110 rule: do not code services that were provided solely in conjunction with a surgical procedure or diagnostic procedure; surgical procedures include routine pre- and post-operative procedures.[^14]
13. If no treatments, procedures, or programs (including isolation) were received or performed within the last 14 days or since admission/entry or reentry, check Z, None of the above, in column b.[^4]
