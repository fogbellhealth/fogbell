## Look-back window

**7 days**[^1]

7-day look-back period ending on the ARD; if the resident's admission/entry or reentry occurred less than 7 days before the ARD, the period is "since admission/entry or reentry." The item table on p. N-4 states the window as "during the last 7 days or since admission/entry or reentry if less than 7 days." The item's own pages alternately use the terms "7-day look-back period" (N0415E1, p. N-7) and "7-day observation period" (general coding instruction, p. N-6) for the same window. Counting convention (ARD + 6 previous calendar days) comes from the manual's general conventions, not from this item's pages.

> **Note:** the manual states more than one time period for this item. See the *Conflicts* section below.

## Coding rules

1. Check Column 1 (N0415E1) if the resident is taking an anticoagulant by pharmacological classification during the 7-day observation period (or since admission/entry or reentry if less than 7 days); Column 2 is checked only if Column 1 is checked and there is an indication noted for all medications in the drug class.[^3]
2. Code all high-risk drug class medications according to their pharmacological classification, not how they are being used.[^4]
3. Code medications in N0415 according to the medication's therapeutic category and/or pharmacological classification, not how it is used.[^5]
4. Medications with more than one therapeutic category and/or pharmacological classification must be coded in all applicable categories/classifications, regardless of how the medication is being used.[^6]
5. Include medications given to the resident by any route in any setting (e.g., at the nursing home, in a hospital emergency room) while a resident of the nursing home.[^7]
6. Do not code antiplatelet medications such as aspirin/extended release, dipyridamole, or clopidogrel as N0415E, Anticoagulant.[^8]
7. Anticoagulants such as Target Specific Oral Anticoagulants (TSOACs), which may or may not require laboratory monitoring, should be coded in N0415E, Anticoagulant.[^9]
8. Do not code flushes given to keep an IV access patent in N0415E, Anticoagulant.[^10]
9. Code a medication even if it was given only once during the look-back period.[^11]
10. Count long-acting medications given every few weeks or monthly only if they are given during the 7-day look-back period (or since admission/entry or reentry if less than 7 days).[^12]
11. For transdermal patches, capture the medication in N0415 only if the transdermal patch was applied to the resident's skin during the observation period.[^13]
12. Combination medications should be coded in all categories/pharmacologic classes that constitute the combination.[^14]
13. Herbal and alternative medicine products are not counted as medications for this item.[^15]
14. Check N0415Z1, None of the above, if none of the listed medications (including anticoagulants) were taken by the resident at any time during the observation period (or since admission/entry or reentry if less than 7 days).[^16]

## What the chart must show

1. Review the resident's medical record for documentation that the medication was received by the resident and for the indication of its use during the 7-day look-back period (or since admission/entry or reentry if less than 7 days). *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^17]
   Both receipt of the anticoagulant and its indication must be documented in the medical record.
2. Review documentation from other health care settings where the resident may have received these medications while a resident of the nursing home (e.g., valium given in the emergency room). *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^18]
3. An "indication" must be an identified, documented clinical rationale for administering the medication, based upon a physician's (or prescriber's) assessment of the resident's condition and therapeutic goals. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^19]
   Definition box governs what satisfies Column 2 (N0415E2).
4. Assessors should be able to identify the source(s) used to support coding the pharmacological classification on the MDS 3.0 (e.g., a consistently used facility resource). *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^20]
   Framed as a suggestion ("may wish to") but includes the expectation that assessors can identify the source supporting coding.
5. Assessors may consult the manufacturer's package insert or work with the resident's pharmacist to confirm the medication's pharmacological classification. *(importance to an auditor: low)*[^21]
   Suggested resource, not required.
6. Providers are responsible for coding each medication's pharmacological/therapeutic classification accurately; use of the listed external resources is not required for MDS completion. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^22]

> **Terminology for the N0415E window: "7-day look-back period" vs. "7-day observation period"**
>
> - *RAI Manual v1.20.1 (Oct 2025):* N0415E1 states the window as the 7-day look-back period (or since admission/entry or reentry if less than 7 days).[^23]
> - *RAI Manual v1.20.1 (Oct 2025):* The general N0415 coding instruction states Column 1 covers the 7-day observation period (or since admission/entry or reentry if less than 7 days).[^24]
>
> Status: Not a substantive difference in length — both describe a 7-day window ending at the ARD; terminology differs only. Recorded for transparency.
