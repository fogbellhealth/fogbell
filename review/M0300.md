**Not stated in the manual for this item.**

Not found for M0300 itself. The M0300 section (pp. M-7 through M-29) instructs the assessor to count ulcers that are "currently present" and does not state a numeric observation window. A 7-day look-back is stated on the preceding gate item M0210 ("Code based on the presence of any pressure ulcer/injury (regardless of stage) in the past 7 days", p. M-5), and p. ## Coding rules

1. For each pressure ulcer, determine the deepest anatomical stage. At admission, code based on findings from the first skin assessment conducted on or after and as close to the admission as possible. Do not reverse or back stage; consider current and historical levels of tissue involvement.[^14]
2. Ulcer staging must be based on the ulcer's deepest anatomic soft tissue damage that is visible or palpable; if the tissues are obscured so depth cannot be observed, the ulcer is unstageable.[^15]
3. If a stageable pressure ulcer has ever been classified at a higher numerical stage than what is observed now, it continues to be classified at the higher numerical stage until healed unless it becomes unstageable.[^16]
4. A previously closed pressure ulcer that opens again is reported at its worst stage, unless it currently presents at a higher stage or is unstageable.[^17]
5. If, after careful cleansing, the ulcer's/injury's anatomical tissues are obscured such that the extent of soft tissue damage cannot be observed or palpated, code it as unstageable.[^18]
6. Pressure ulcers with eschar (tan, black, or brown) or slough (yellow, tan, gray, green or brown) present such that anatomic depth cannot be visualized or palpated in the wound bed are classified as unstageable.[^19]
7. If the wound bed is only partially covered by eschar or slough and the anatomical depth of tissue damage can be visualized or palpated, numerically stage the ulcer and do not code it as unstageable.[^20]
8. A pressure injury with intact skin that is a deep tissue injury (DTI) must not be coded as a Stage 1 pressure injury; code it as unstageable.[^21]
9. Known pressure ulcers/injuries covered by a non-removable dressing/device are coded as unstageable; "known" requires documentation that a pressure ulcer/injury exists under the dressing/device.[^22]
10. Present on admission is determined for each ulcer/injury: it must have been present at the time of admission/entry or reentry and not acquired while the resident was in the care of the nursing home. "On admission" means as close to the actual time of admission as possible.[^23]
11. If an ulcer/injury present on admission/entry or reentry subsequently increases in numerical stage during the stay, code it at the higher stage and do not consider that higher stage "present on admission."[^24]
12. If a numerically stageable ulcer/injury present on admission/entry or reentry becomes unstageable due to slough or eschar during the stay, code it at M0300F and do not code it as "present on admission."[^25]
13. If an ulcer/injury unstageable on admission/entry or reentry later becomes numerically stageable, consider it "present on admission" at the stage at which it first becomes numerically stageable; a subsequent increase in numerical stage is not coded as present on admission.[^26]
14. An ulcer/injury originally acquired in the facility that returns from hospitalization at the same numerical stage is not coded as "present on admission."[^27]
15. An ulcer/injury that was "present on admission" (not facility acquired) and returns from hospitalization at the same numerical stage is still coded as "present on admission."[^28]
16. If an ulcer/injury increases in numerical stage or becomes unstageable due to slough or eschar during a hospitalization, code it as "present on admission" upon reentry.[^29]
17. If an ulcer was numerically staged, became unstageable, and is later debrided enough to be numerically staged, compare stages before and after; if the numerical stage increased, code it as not "present on admission."[^30]
18. An ulcer/injury documented on admission that then closed and reopens at the same stage (not a higher stage) is coded as "present on admission."[^31]
19. If two pressure ulcers that were both "present on admission" merge, continue to code the merged ulcer as "present on admission"; increased surface area alone does not change this—there must be an increase in numerical stage or a change to unstageable due to slough or eschar.[^32]
20. If an ulcer/injury unstageable on admission/entry or reentry becomes unstageable for another reason, it is still considered "present on admission" at the new unstageable status.[^33]
21. For each M0300 stage/unstageable category, determine that the lesion being assessed is primarily related to pressure and that other conditions have been ruled out; if pressure is not the primary cause, do not code here.[^34]
22. Skip pattern: if M0300B1 is 0 (no Stage 2 pressure ulcers present), enter 0 and skip to M0300C, Stage 3.[^35]
23. Skip pattern: if M0300C1 is 0, enter 0 and skip to M0300D, Stage 4.[^36]
24. Skip pattern: if M0300D1 is 0, enter 0 and skip to M0300E, Unstageable – Non-removable dressing.[^37]
25. Skip pattern: if M0300E1 is 0, enter 0 and skip to M0300F, Unstageable – Slough and/or eschar.[^38]
26. Skip pattern: if M0300F1 is 0, enter 0 and skip to M0300G, Unstageable – Deep tissue injury.[^39]
27. Skip pattern: if M0300G1 is 0, enter 0 and skip to M1030, Number of Venous and Arterial Ulcers.[^40]
28. Stage 2 exclusions: do not code skin tears, tape burns, moisture associated skin damage, or excoriation at M0300B; granulation tissue, slough and eschar are not present in Stage 2 pressure ulcers.[^41]
29. When a pressure ulcer presents as an intact blister and a deep tissue injury is determined from adjacent/surrounding tissue, do not code it as a Stage 2.[^42]
30. Stage 3: bone/tendon/muscle is not visible or directly palpable; do not code moisture-associated skin damage or excoriation at M0300C.[^43]
31. Stage 4: exposed bone/tendon/muscle is visible or directly palpable; because cartilage serves the same anatomical function as bone, pressure ulcers with exposed cartilage are classified as Stage 4.[^44]
32. Once a pressure ulcer is debrided of slough and/or eschar such that the anatomic depth of soft tissue damage can be determined, code the ulcer for the reclassified stage; it does not have to be completely debrided or free of all slough/eschar for reclassification.[^45]
33. Once a deep tissue injury has opened to an ulcer, reclassify the ulcer into the appropriate stage and code it for the reclassified stage.[^46]
34. When a lesion due to pressure presents with an intact blister AND the surrounding or adjacent soft tissue does NOT have the characteristics of deep tissue injury, do not code it at M0300G.[^47]
35. Do not code pressure ulcers/injuries coded in M0210 through M0300 at M1030 (venous/arterial ulcers) or at M1040 (other ulcers, wounds and skin problems).[^48]
36. If both pressure and moisture are present, or if tissue damage extends into the subcutaneous tissue or deeper and/or necrosis is present, code the skin damage as a pressure ulcer/injury in M0300 rather than as MASD.[^49]

## What the chart must show

1. Review the history of each pressure ulcer in the medical record; careful facility documentation and tracking of pressure ulcers supports accurate coding of this item. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^50]
   Basis for stage-history and back-staging determinations.
2. Facilities may document the healing of pressure ulcers using descriptive characteristics of the wound (depth, width, presence or absence of granulation tissue) or by using a validated pressure ulcer healing tool. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^51]
   Stated as permitted methods rather than a mandate.
3. To code an ulcer/injury as unstageable under a non-removable dressing/device, documentation must be available stating that a pressure ulcer/injury exists under that dressing/device. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^52]
   Documentation is a precondition for coding M0300E.
4. For present-on-admission determinations, review the medical record for the history of the ulcer/injury and for its location and stage at the time of admission/entry or reentry. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^53]
5. For M0300E, review the medical record for documentation of a pressure ulcer/injury covered by a non-removable dressing/device, including physician's orders indicating the dressing or orthopedic device is not to be removed. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^54]
6. For deep tissue injury, clearly document assessment findings in the resident's medical record and track and document appropriate wound care planning and management. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^55]
7. Examine the resident in a well-lit room and, for any pressure ulcers/injuries identified, measure and record the deepest anatomical stage (assessment step preceding M0300 staging). *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^56]
   Stated under M0210 Steps for Assessment, which feeds M0300 staging.
