# Questions for the Verifier

These are the things the manual does not settle and we cannot decide from a desk. Page numbers are the manual's own printed page labels (RAI Manual v1.20.1, October 2025).

1. **Mood items: which window counts?** The manual says the staff mood interview is conducted "during the 7-day look-back period based on the ARD" (p. D-13), but the questions themselves ask about "the last 2 weeks" (p. D-3 for the resident interview, p. D-12 for the staff assessment). When a case-mix reviewer checks the chart for evidence behind a mood item, which window do they check against: the 7 days, or the 2 weeks? And how does Maine's 28-day mood guidance relate to either of these? For now the rulebook uses the 7 days and flags the 2 weeks as a note; your answer replaces that.

2. **"Assessment period" versus "look-back period".** The resident-interview score page (D0160, p. D-10) talks about symptoms "during the assessment period"; the staff-assessment score page (D0600, p. D-17) says "during the look-back period" for the same thresholds. In practice, are these the same thing?

3. **Section E behavior items: how are the 7 days counted?** Every Section E item says "in the last 7 days" (for example p. E-2, p. E-5), but the Section E pages never say how those days are counted relative to the assessment reference date. Is it the ARD plus the six days before it, the same way as elsewhere? Is there anywhere in the manual or the state's guidance that spells this out for behavior items?

4. **Does the audit look at the individual mood symptoms?** The staff mood assessment (D0500) has ten lettered symptoms, A through J. Does Maine's case-mix review look at the chart evidence for each lettered symptom on its own, or only at the totals (D0500 overall and the D0600 score)? This decides whether we keep a separate page for each letter.

5. **Standing question: which instrument does Maine's case-mix audit validate today?** CMS removed the old Section G (the G0110 ADL items and their neighbors) from the federal MDS in October 2023; they survive only in the Optional State Assessment for states that kept a RUG-based system. Does Maine's case-mix review still validate the Section G items through the Optional State Assessment, or has it moved to the Section GG items, or something else? And what did the state tell facilities to do when CMS announced the Optional State Assessment's retirement?

6. **Which diagnoses matter for Maine's case-mix?** The manual's Section I checklist has about sixty conditions and never says which ones drive payment. We pulled eleven that the RUG and PDPM nursing groupings are known to use: primary condition category (I0020), pneumonia (I2000), septicemia (I2100), diabetes (I2900), cerebral palsy (I4400), hemiplegia or hemiparesis (I4900), quadriplegia (I5100), multiple sclerosis (I5200), Parkinson's (I5300), asthma/COPD (I6200), and respiratory failure (I6300). Does Maine's case-mix review look at others, for example depression (I5800), Alzheimer's or other dementia (I4200, I4800), or hip fracture (I3900)? Any we should drop?

7. **Special treatments and nutrition: which columns and rows does the audit check?** O0110 (special treatments, p. O-1 to O-2) and K0520 (nutritional approaches, p. K-11) each have several columns with different time windows: days 1 to 3 of a Medicare stay, "while a resident" in the last 7 or 14 days, and the last 3 days before discharge. For a Maine case-mix review, which column is checked against the chart, and which rows matter most (for example tube feeding K0520B, IV feeding K0520A, tracheostomy O0110E1, ventilator O0110F1, isolation O0110M1)?

8. **Where does the 28-day mood guidance live?** We read both public MaineCare rules for nursing facilities: Chapter II §67 (Nursing Facility Services, last updated 2014) and Chapter III §67 (Principles of Reimbursement, amended April 2025). Neither mentions a 28-day period, mood, or the PHQ. If Maine's reviewers use a 28-day window for mood, which document says so: a provider bulletin, a case-mix review manual, reviewer training materials, or something else? Anything you can name or send lets us cite it properly.

9. **Does §22.2 mean the audit now checks GG rather than G?** Chapter III §22.2 (2025) says Maine's direct care rate uses "only the Nursing Component of the CMS Skilled Nursing Home Patient Driven Payment Model," classifying residents into 26 groups from MDS 3.0 items including "the patient's functional score." In PDPM that functional score comes from Section GG, not the old Section G. Is that how the case-mix reviewers work today, or is there still a Section G / Optional State Assessment step we are not seeing?

10. **Does the eligibility Behavior Screen ever touch the case-mix review?** Chapter II defines "Problem Behavior" for nursing-facility eligibility as wandering, verbal abuse, physical abuse, or disruptive behavior scoring 2 or 3 "at least four (4) days per week" (§67.01-20), and scores it on the state's MED form, which the rule says is not the MDS (§67.01-17). When a reviewer checks the chart behind E0200 or E0900 on the MDS, does that eligibility threshold, or the MED form, ever come into it?

11. **How big is the review sample in practice?** You described the case-mix review as roughly ten percent of Medicaid records each quarter. The current reimbursement rule says "a record sample of twenty-four percent (24%) with a minimum of five (5) records shall be drawn from MDS assessments completed for residents who have MaineCare reimbursement" (Chapter III §16.2.3.3(3), amended April 2025). Which reflects what facilities actually see today? Your memory may reflect an earlier version of the rule or the practical reality on the ground; either way the difference is worth knowing.
