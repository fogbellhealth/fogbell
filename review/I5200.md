## Look-back window

**7 days**[^1]

The item's own section pages state two look-back periods for I0100–I8000: diagnosis identification (Step 1) is a 60-day look-back period, and diagnosis status (active or inactive, Step 2) is a 7-day look-back period. The assessor's observation window for determining whether the multiple sclerosis diagnosis is ACTIVE is 7 days (ARD plus the 6 prior calendar days, per the general convention at p. 2-17/2-21). The separate 60-day window applies only to identifying that a physician-documented diagnosis exists (p. I-8). The 30-day exception stated on p. I-8/I-9/I-14 applies to Item I2300 UTI only, not to I5200.

> **Note:** the manual states more than one time period for this item. See the *Conflicts* section below.

## Coding rules

1. Code diseases that have a documented diagnosis in the last 60 days and have a direct relationship to the resident's current functional status, cognitive status, mood or behavior status, medical treatments, nursing monitoring, or risk of death during the 7-day look-back period.[^4]
2. Check off each active disease; check all that apply.[^5]
3. Do not include conditions that have been resolved, do not affect the resident's current status, or do not drive the resident's plan of care during the 7-day look-back period; these are inactive diagnoses.[^6]
4. The disease conditions in this section require a physician-documented diagnosis (or by a nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or clinical nurse specialist if allowable under state licensure laws) in the last 60 days.[^7]
5. Listing a disease/diagnosis on the resident's medical record problem list is not sufficient for determining active or inactive status.[^8]
6. If a diagnosis/problem list is used as a source, only diagnoses confirmed by the physician should be entered.[^9]
7. Examples of diseases listed for a category are not exhaustive; diseases to be coded in these categories are not meant to be limited to only those listed in the examples.[^10]
8. If a disease or condition is not specifically listed among I0100–I7900, enter the diagnosis and ICD code in item I8000, Additional active diagnosis.[^11]
9. Ongoing therapy with medications or other interventions to manage a condition that requires monitoring for therapeutic efficacy or for potentially severe side effects in the last 7 days indicates active disease; nurses' routine monitoring of medications for adverse effects (pharmacotherapy management) does not by itself indicate management or monitoring of the underlying disease.[^12]
10. Where practitioners have potentially misdiagnosed a resident with a condition for which there is a lack of appropriate diagnostic information in the medical record, the corresponding diagnosis in Section I should not be coded.[^13]
11. If the primary medical condition coded in I0020B is multiple sclerosis (a progressive neurological condition, I0020 code 06), that primary medical condition must also be included in items I0100–I8000.[^14]

## What the chart must show

1. A physician-documented diagnosis of multiple sclerosis (or by a nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or clinical nurse specialist if allowable under state licensure laws) exists in the medical record within the last 60 days. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^15]
   Diagnosis identification step (Step 1).
2. Medical record sources for the physician diagnosis include progress notes, the most recent history and physical, transfer documents, discharge summaries, diagnosis/problem list, and other resources as available. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^16]
3. Diagnoses communicated verbally, and diagnostic information including past history obtained from family members and close contacts, must be documented in the medical record by the physician. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^17]
4. Documentation in the last 7 days from the medical record establishing active status: transfer documents, physician progress notes, recent history and physical, recent discharge summaries, nursing assessments, nursing care plans, medication sheets, doctor's orders, consults and official diagnostic reports. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^18]
   Active status step (Step 2).
5. Specific documentation by a physician, nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or clinical nurse specialist that the condition is active is sufficient and requires no additional confirmation. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^19]
   Framed as an indicator that may assist assessors.
6. In the absence of specific documentation that the disease is active, documentation of recent onset or acute exacerbation indicated by a positive study, test or procedure, hospitalization for acute symptoms, and/or recent change in therapy in the last 7 days may confirm active disease (sources: radiological reports, hospital discharge summaries, doctor's orders). *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^20]
7. In the absence of specific documentation, symptoms and abnormal signs indicating ongoing or decompensated disease in the last 7 days may confirm active disease; a symptom must be specifically attributed to the disease (sources: radiological reports, nursing assessments and care plans, progress notes). *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^21]
8. In the absence of specific documentation, documentation of ongoing therapy with medications or other interventions in the last 7 days to manage the condition that requires monitoring for therapeutic efficacy or for potentially severe side effects may confirm active disease. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^22]
9. Where the problem list is the only source, the reviewer should check progress notes (including the history and physical) during the 7-day look-back period for treatment of symptoms, doctor's orders for medications, and documentation of physical or other therapy for functional limitations caused by the condition. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^23]
   Stated for arthritis as an example, but presented as the general method for determining active status.

## ⚠️ Conflicts — please read carefully

The manual (or two documents) say different things here. Both sides are shown; nothing has been dropped.

> **Two coexisting time windows apply to coding I5200 (and all I0100–I8000 diagnosis items): a 60-day diagnosis-identification window and a 7-day active-status window.**
>
> - *Federal manual:* Diagnosis identification (Step 1) uses a 60-day look-back period; a physician-documented diagnosis must exist in the last 60 days.[^24]
> - *Federal manual:* Diagnosis status (active or inactive, Step 2) uses a 7-day look-back period.[^1]
>
> Status: Resolved by the document itself: the manual explicitly states that both periods apply to this section, each to a different assessment step. The assessor's observation window for active status — and therefore lookback_days — is 7 days; the 60-day window governs only whether a physician-documented diagnosis exists.
