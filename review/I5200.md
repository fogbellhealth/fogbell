## Look-back window

**7 days**[^1]

I5200 is one of the checkboxes in the I0100–I8000 "Active Diagnoses in the Last 7 Days" section. The document states two look-back periods for this section: diagnosis identification (Step 1) uses a 60-day look-back for the physician-documented diagnosis, and diagnosis status (active/inactive, Step 2) uses a 7-day look-back. The assessor's observation window for determining active status is 7 days; the 60-day window governs identification of the physician-documented diagnosis. The document does not state on these pages how the 7-day window is counted relative to the ARD.

## Coding rules

1. I5200 is the Neurological-category checkbox for multiple sclerosis (MS) within items I0100–I8000, Active Diagnoses in the Last 7 Days.[^4]
2. Code diseases that have a documented diagnosis in the last 60 days AND have a direct relationship to the resident's current functional status, cognitive status, mood or behavior status, medical treatments, nursing monitoring, or risk of death during the 7-day look-back period.[^5]
3. The diagnosis must be physician-documented (or documented by a nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or clinical nurse specialist if allowable under state licensure laws) within the last 60 days.[^6]
4. Once the diagnosis is identified, the assessor must determine whether it is active; do not code conditions that have been resolved, do not affect the resident's current status, or do not drive the plan of care during the 7-day look-back period.[^3]
5. Check all that apply — each active disease is checked off; multiple diagnosis checkboxes may be coded on the same assessment.[^2]
6. If the primary medical condition coded in I0020B is multiple sclerosis, it must also be included (checked) in items I0100–I8000.[^7]
7. Listing the diagnosis on the resident's medical record problem list alone is not sufficient to determine active or inactive status.[^8]
8. Routine nursing monitoring of medications for adverse effects does not by itself establish active disease; for coding purposes that monitoring relates to management of pharmacotherapy, not to management or monitoring of the underlying disease.[^9]
9. Where a practitioner has potentially misdiagnosed the resident and appropriate diagnostic information is lacking in the medical record, the corresponding Section I diagnosis should not be coded.[^10]
10. If aftercare following hospitalization is coded with a Z code, another diagnosis for the related primary medical condition must still be checked in items I0100–I7900 or entered in I8000.[^11]

## What the chart must show

1. A physician-documented diagnosis of multiple sclerosis dated within the last 60 days; acceptable medical record sources include progress notes, the most recent history and physical, transfer documents, discharge summaries, and the diagnosis/problem list (problem-list entries only if confirmed by the physician). *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^12]
2. Diagnoses communicated verbally by the physician must be documented in the medical record by the physician. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^13]
3. Diagnostic information, including past history obtained from family members and close contacts, must be documented in the medical record by the physician. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^14]
4. Evidence of active status must be sought in medical record sources covering the last 7 days: transfer documents, physician progress notes, recent history and physical, recent discharge summaries, nursing assessments, nursing care plans, medication sheets, doctor's orders, consults and official diagnostic reports. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^15]
5. Specific documentation by a physician, nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or clinical nurse specialist that the condition is active (e.g., in progress notes, most recent history and physical, transfer notes, hospital discharge summary) is sufficient and requires no additional confirmation. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^16]
   Framed as an indicator that "may assist assessors"; documentation of active status is nonetheless the basis for coding.
6. In the absence of specific documentation that the disease is active: documentation of recent onset or acute exacerbation indicated by a positive study, test or procedure, hospitalization for acute symptoms, and/or recent change in therapy in the last 7 days (sources: radiological reports, hospital discharge summaries, doctor's orders). *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^17]
7. Documentation of symptoms and abnormal signs indicating ongoing or decompensated disease in the last 7 days, with the symptom specifically attributed to the disease (sources: radiological reports, nursing assessments and care plans, progress notes). *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^18]
   The document adds: "a symptom must be specifically attributed to the disease."
8. Documentation of ongoing therapy with medications or other interventions in the last 7 days that manage the condition and require monitoring for therapeutic efficacy or for potentially severe side effects. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^19]

> - *Federal manual:* Diagnosis identification (Step 1) uses a 60-day look-back period.[^20]

>
