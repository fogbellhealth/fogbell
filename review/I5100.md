## Look-back window

**7 days**[^1]

The item's own section states two look-back periods: diagnosis identification (Step 1) is a 60-day look-back period, and diagnosis status (active or inactive, Step 2) is a 7-day look-back period. The observation window against which active status is judged is 7 days (ARD plus the 6 prior calendar days per the general convention at p. 2-17/2-21), so the look-back window = 7; the 60-day window applies only to identifying that a physician-documented diagnosis exists. The I2300 UTI exception (30 days) does not apply to I5100.

> **Note:** the manual states more than one time period for this item. See the *Conflicts* section below.

## Coding rules

1. Code diseases that have a documented diagnosis in the last 60 days and have a direct relationship to the resident's current functional status, cognitive status, mood or behavior status, medical treatments, nursing monitoring, or risk of death during the 7-day look-back period.[^4]
2. The disease conditions in this section require a physician-documented diagnosis (or by a nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or clinical nurse specialist if allowable under state licensure laws) in the last 60 days.[^5]
3. Do not include conditions that have been resolved, do not affect the resident's current status, or do not drive the resident's plan of care during the 7-day look-back period; these are inactive diagnoses.[^6]
4. Check off each active disease; check all that apply.[^2]
5. Coding I5100 Quadriplegia is limited to spinal cord injuries and must be a primary diagnosis and not the result of another condition.[^7]
6. For residents with functional quadriplegia (complete immobility due to severe physical disability or frailty, e.g., from cerebral palsy, stroke, contractures, brain disease, advanced dementia), code the primary physician-documented diagnosis on the MDS and not the resulting paralysis or paresis; e.g., cerebral palsy with spastic quadriplegia is coded in I4400 Cerebral Palsy, not in I5100.[^8]
7. Quadriplegia primarily refers to the paralysis of all four limbs, arms and legs, caused by spinal cord injury.[^9]
8. Listing a disease/diagnosis on the resident's medical record problem list is not sufficient for determining active or inactive status.[^10]
9. Ongoing therapy with medications or other interventions to manage a condition that requires monitoring for therapeutic efficacy or for potentially severe side effects in the last 7 days indicates active disease; monitoring of medications for adverse effects alone (management of pharmacotherapy) does not.[^11]
10. Where practitioners have potentially misdiagnosed a resident with a condition for which there is a lack of appropriate diagnostic information in the medical record, the corresponding diagnosis in Section I should not be coded.[^12]
11. If a disease or condition is not specifically listed among the checkbox items, enter the diagnosis and ICD code in item I8000, Additional active diagnoses.[^13]
12. When the primary medical condition is coded in item I0020B, include that condition in items I0100–I8000: Active Diagnoses in the Last 7 Days.[^14]

## What the chart must show

1. Physician-documented diagnosis of quadriplegia within the last 60 days in the medical record; sources include progress notes, most recent history and physical, transfer documents, discharge summaries, diagnosis/problem list, and other resources as available. If a diagnosis/problem list is used, only diagnoses confirmed by the physician should be entered. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^15]
   Applies to all Section I diagnosis items, including I5100.
2. Diagnoses communicated verbally, and diagnostic information/past history obtained from family members or close contacts, must be documented in the medical record by the physician. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^16]
3. Documentation from the last 7 days establishing active status — transfer documents, physician progress notes, recent history and physical, recent discharge summaries, nursing assessments, nursing care plans, medication sheets, doctor's orders, consults and official diagnostic reports, and other sources as available. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^17]
4. Documentation that the quadriplegia is due to spinal cord injury and is a primary diagnosis (not paralysis resulting from another condition such as cerebral palsy, stroke, contractures, brain disease, or advanced dementia). *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^7]
   Item-specific coding tip for I5100.
5. Specific documentation in the medical record by a physician, nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or clinical nurse specialist that the diagnosis is active (e.g., in progress notes, most recent history and physical, transfer notes, hospital discharge summary) requires no additional confirmation. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^18]
   Framed as indicators that "may assist assessors" rather than as a mandatory documentation form.
6. In the absence of specific documentation that the disease is active, documentation of recent onset/acute exacerbation (positive study, test or procedure, hospitalization for acute symptoms, or recent change in therapy in the last 7 days), of symptoms and abnormal signs indicating ongoing or decompensated disease in the last 7 days, or of ongoing medication/interventions requiring monitoring in the last 7 days. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^19]
   Listed as permissive indicators ("may be used").

> **Two look-back windows stated for Section I active diagnoses (60 days for diagnosis identification vs. 7 days for active status)**
>
> - *RAI Manual v1.20.1 (Oct 2025):* Diagnosis identification (Step 1) uses a 60-day look-back period.[^20]
> - *RAI Manual v1.20.1 (Oct 2025):* Diagnosis status (active or inactive, Step 2) uses a 7-day look-back period.[^1]
>
> Status: Resolved by the document itself: both windows apply in sequence — a physician-documented diagnosis within 60 days plus a direct relationship to current status during the 7-day look-back period (p. I-9). lookback_days records the 7-day active-status observation window.
