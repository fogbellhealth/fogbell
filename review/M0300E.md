## Coding rules

1. M0300E1: Enter the number of pressure ulcers/injuries that are unstageable related to non-removable dressing/device.[^3]
2. M0300E1: Enter 0 if no unstageable pressure ulcers/injuries related to non-removable dressing/device are present, and skip to M0300F, Unstageable – Slough and/or eschar.[^4]
3. M0300E2: Enter the number of these unstageable pressure ulcers/injuries related to a non-removable dressing/device that were first noted at the time of admission/entry AND—for residents reentering after a hospital stay—that were acquired during the hospitalization (i.e., not acquired in the nursing facility prior to admission to the hospital).[^5]
4. M0300E2: Enter 0 if no unstageable pressure ulcers/injuries related to non-removable dressing/device were first noted at the time of admission/entry or reentry.[^6]
5. Known pressure ulcers/injuries covered by a non-removable dressing/device (e.g., primary surgical dressing, cast) are coded as unstageable; "Known" means documentation is available that says a pressure ulcer/injury exists under the non-removable dressing/device.[^7]
6. Present on admission determination (applies to each pressure ulcer/injury, including those coded at M0300E): determine if the ulcer/injury was present at the time of admission/entry or reentry and not acquired while the resident was in the care of the nursing home; consider current and historical levels of tissue involvement.[^8]
7. If a pressure ulcer/injury was unstageable on admission/entry or reentry and then becomes numerically stageable later, it is considered "present on admission" at the stage at which it first becomes numerically stageable; a subsequent increase in numerical stage is not coded as present on admission.[^9]
8. If a pressure ulcer/injury was unstageable on admission/entry or reentry and then becomes unstageable for another reason, it is considered "present on admission" at the new unstageable status.[^10]
9. A resident admitted with documentation that a pressure ulcer/injury is present under a cast that cannot be removed is coded on the Admission assessment as an unstageable pressure ulcer/injury due to non-removable dressing/device (M0300E1 = 1 and M0300E2 = 1, present on admission/entry or reentry).[^11]
10. When a known ulcer/injury under a non-removable dressing/device is coded unstageable and present on admission at M0300E, and the dressing is later removed revealing a numerical stage that subsequently worsens, the higher stage is not coded as present on admission (example: M0300E1 = 1 and M0300E2 = 1 on admission; on the subsequent assessment M0300C1 = 1 and M0300C2 = 0).[^12]

## What the chart must show

1. Medical record contains documentation that a pressure ulcer/injury is present under the non-removable dressing/device (this documentation is what makes the ulcer/injury "known" and codeable as unstageable). *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^13]
   Directly conditions whether the ulcer/injury may be counted at M0300E1.
2. Review of the medical record for documentation of a pressure ulcer/injury covered by a non-removable dressing/device. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^14]
   Stated as Step 1 of the assessment process for this item.
3. Documentation identifying the device as non-removable (e.g., a dressing or orthopedic device that is not to be removed per physician's order, or a cast). *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^15]
   Framed as examples within the assessment steps.
4. Medical record review for the history, location, and stage of the ulcer/injury at the time of admission/entry or reentry to support the "present on admission" coding at M0300E2. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^16]
   Part of Step 3, Determine "Present on Admission," which M0300E's assessment steps cross-reference.
