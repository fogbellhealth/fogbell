## Look-back window

**7 days**[^1]

I2100 falls under the I0100–I8000 Active Diagnoses in the Last 7 Days section, which states two look-back periods: diagnosis identification (Step 1) is a 60-day look-back, and diagnosis status (active or inactive, Step 2) is a 7-day look-back. The 7-day active-status window is recorded as the assessor's observation window; the 60-day window applies to identifying the physician-documented diagnosis. The only stated exception to the 7-day active look-back is I2300 UTI, not I2100.

> **Note:** the manual states more than one time period for this item. See the *Conflicts* section below.

## Coding rules

1. For sepsis to be considered septicemia, there must be inflammation due to sepsis and evidence of a microbial process.[^4]
2. If the medical record reflects inflammation due to sepsis and evidence of a microbial process, code I2100, Septicemia.[^2]
3. If the medical record does not reflect inflammation due to sepsis and evidence of a microbial process, enter the sepsis diagnosis and ICD code in item I8000, Additional Active Diagnoses, rather than coding I2100.[^3]
4. Code the disease only if it has a documented diagnosis in the last 60 days AND has a direct relationship to the resident's current functional status, cognitive status, mood or behavior status, medical treatments, nursing monitoring, or risk of death during the 7-day look-back period.[^5]
5. The disease condition requires a physician-documented diagnosis (or by a nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or clinical nurse specialist if allowable under state licensure laws) in the last 60 days.[^6]
6. Do not include conditions that have been resolved, do not affect the resident's current status, or do not drive the resident's plan of care during the 7-day look-back period; these are inactive diagnoses.[^7]
7. Check off each active disease; check all that apply.[^8]
8. Diagnoses listed in parentheses in the item set are provided as examples and should not be considered all-inclusive lists.[^9]
9. If the primary medical condition coded in I0020B is septicemia, it must also be coded in items I0100–I8000: Active Diagnoses in the Last 7 Days.[^10]
10. Where practitioners have potentially misdiagnosed a resident with a condition for which there is a lack of appropriate diagnostic information in the medical record, the corresponding Section I diagnosis should not be coded.[^11]

## What the chart must show

1. Medical record documentation reflecting inflammation due to sepsis and evidence of a microbial process. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^4]
   Item-specific prerequisite for coding I2100 rather than I8000.
2. Physician-documented diagnosis (or NP, PA, or clinical nurse specialist if allowable under state licensure laws) within the last 60 days; sources include progress notes, most recent history and physical, transfer documents, discharge summaries, diagnosis/problem list. If a diagnosis/problem list is used, only diagnoses confirmed by the physician should be entered. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^12]
3. Diagnoses communicated verbally must be documented in the medical record by the physician; diagnostic information including past history obtained from family members and close contacts must also be documented in the medical record by the physician. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^13]
4. Evidence of active status within the last 7 days from medical record sources: transfer documents, physician progress notes, recent history and physical, recent discharge summaries, nursing assessments, nursing care plans, medication sheets, doctor's orders, consults and official diagnostic reports. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^14]
5. Specific documentation by a physician, nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or clinical nurse specialist that the diagnosis is active is sufficient and requires no additional confirmation. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^15]
   Framed as an indicator that "may assist assessors."
6. In the absence of specific documentation that the disease is active, evidence of recent onset or acute exacerbation (positive study, test or procedure, hospitalization for acute symptoms, and/or recent change in therapy in the last 7 days), from sources such as radiological reports, hospital discharge summaries, and doctor's orders. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^16]
   Framed as an indicator that may be used to confirm active disease.
7. In the absence of specific documentation that the disease is active, documented symptoms and abnormal signs indicating ongoing or decompensated disease in the last 7 days, with the symptom specifically attributed to the disease. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^17]
8. In the absence of specific documentation that the disease is active, documentation of ongoing therapy with medications or other interventions to manage the condition requiring monitoring for therapeutic efficacy or severe side effects in the last 7 days. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^18]
9. A diagnosis listed only on the resident's medical record problem list is not sufficient for determining active or inactive status. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^19]
10. Routine nursing monitoring of medications for adverse effects does not by itself support active disease; that monitoring relates to management of pharmacotherapy, not management or monitoring of the underlying disease. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^20]
