## Look-back window

**7 days**[^1]

The assessor's observation window for Columns 2 and 3 is the 7-day look-back period (Column 2 = performed while NOT a resident and within the last 7 days; Column 3 = performed while a resident and within the last 7 days). The document states additional, column-specific windows: Column 1 assessment period is days 1 through 3 of the SNF PPS Stay starting with A2400B, and Column 4 assessment period is the last 3 days of the SNF PPS Stay ending on A2400C. Column 2 is left blank if the resident was admitted/entered or reentered more than 7 days ago.

> **Note:** the manual states more than one time period for this item. See the *Conflicts* section below.

## Coding rules

1. Check all that apply for K0520; if none apply, check K0520Z, None of the above.[^5]
2. K0520A is the item for parenteral/IV feeding.[^6]
3. Parenteral/IV feeding is the introduction of a nutritive substance into the body by means other than the intestinal tract (e.g., subcutaneous, intravenous).[^7]
4. Column 1: check parenteral/IV feeding if performed during the first 3 days of the SNF PPS Stay.[^2]
5. Column 2: check parenteral/IV feeding if performed prior to admission/entry or reentry to the facility and within the 7-day look-back period; leave Column 2 blank if the resident was admitted/entered or reentered the facility more than 7 days ago.[^3]
6. When completing the Interim Payment Assessment (IPA), completion of K0520A (along with K0520B and K0520Z) is required.[^8]
7. Column 3: check parenteral/IV feeding if performed after admission/entry or reentry to the facility and within the 7-day look-back period.[^1]
8. Column 4: check parenteral/IV feeding if performed within the last 3 days of the SNF PPS Stay.[^4]
9. K0520A includes any and all nutrition and hydration received by the nursing home resident during the observation period either at the nursing home, at the hospital as an outpatient or an inpatient, provided they were administered for nutrition or hydration.[^9]
10. IV fluids or hyperalimentation, including total parenteral nutrition (TPN), administered continuously or intermittently, may be included in K0520A when there is supporting documentation of the need for additional fluid intake specifically addressing a nutrition or hydration need.[^10]
11. IV fluids running at KVO (Keep Vein Open) may be included in K0520A when supported by documentation of a nutrition or hydration need.[^11]
12. IV fluids contained in IV Piggybacks may be included in K0520A when supported by documentation of a nutrition or hydration need.[^12]
13. Hypodermoclysis and subcutaneous ports in hydration therapy may be included in K0520A when supported by documentation of a nutrition or hydration need.[^13]
14. IV fluids can be coded in K0520A if needed to prevent dehydration when the additional fluid intake is specifically needed for nutrition and/or hydration; prevention of dehydration should be clinically indicated and supporting documentation should be provided in the medical record.[^14]
15. Do not code IV medications in K0520A; code these when appropriate in O0110H, IV Medications.[^15]
16. Do not code IV fluids used to reconstitute and/or dilute medications for IV administration in K0520A.[^16]
17. Do not code IV fluids administered as a routine part of an operative or diagnostic procedure or recovery room stay in K0520A.[^17]
18. Do not code IV fluids administered solely as flushes in K0520A.[^18]
19. Do not code parenteral/IV fluids administered in conjunction with chemotherapy or dialysis in K0520A.[^19]
20. Complete K0710 only if Column 2 and/or Column 3 are checked for K0520A and/or K0520B.[^20]

## What the chart must show

1. Supporting documentation in the resident's medical record reflecting the need for additional fluid intake specifically addressing a nutrition or hydration need, noted according to State and Federal Regulations and/or internal facility policy, before including the listed fluids in K0520A. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^21]
   Applies to the bulleted list of includable fluids (TPN/hyperalimentation, KVO, piggybacks, hypodermoclysis/subcutaneous ports).
2. When IV fluids are coded to prevent dehydration, prevention of dehydration should be clinically indicated and supporting documentation provided in the medical record. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^22]
3. Medical record review is used to determine whether the nutritional approach was performed during the look-back period. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^23]
4. Absent documentation supporting a need for additional fluid intake, K0520A is not checked even though the resident received additional fluid (example rationale). *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^24]
   Illustrative example (Resident J); K0520A1 would NOT be checked.
5. Documentation of a physician-ordered additional volume of dilutant to address inadequate oral fluid intake supports checking K0520A columns 1, 2, and 3 (example rationale). *(importance to an auditor: low)*[^25]

> **Coexistence of multiple time windows for K0520A (7-day look-back vs. 3-day SNF PPS Stay windows)**
>
> - *Federal manual:* Columns 2 and 3 use a 7-day look-back period.[^1]
> - *Federal manual:* Column 1 uses the first 3 days of the SNF PPS Stay and Column 4 uses the last 3 days of the SNF PPS Stay.[^4]
>
> Status: Resolved by the document itself: the applicable window is column-specific (Columns 1 and 4 = 3-day SNF PPS Stay windows; Columns 2 and 3 = 7-day look-back).
