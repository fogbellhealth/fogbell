## Look-back window

**7 days**[^1]

Two look-back periods apply to the I0100–I8000 section that contains I6200: diagnosis identification is a 60-day look-back (physician-documented diagnosis in the last 60 days), and diagnosis active/inactive status is a 7-day look-back. The 7-day window is the assessor's observation window for determining active status and is recorded in the look-back window; the 60-day diagnosis-identification window is stated on p. I-8 ("require a physician-documented diagnosis ... in the last 60 days"). The 30-day exception applies only to I2300 UTI, not to I6200.

> **Note:** the manual states more than one time period for this item. See the *Conflicts* section below.

## Coding rules

1. Code diseases that have a documented diagnosis in the last 60 days and have a direct relationship to the resident's current functional status, cognitive status, mood or behavior status, medical treatments, nursing monitoring, or risk of death during the 7-day look-back period.[^4]
2. The disease conditions in this section require a physician-documented diagnosis (or by a nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or clinical nurse specialist if allowable under state licensure laws) in the last 60 days.[^5]
3. Once a diagnosis is identified it must be determined whether it is active; do not include conditions that have been resolved, do not affect the resident's current status, or do not drive the resident's plan of care during the 7-day look-back period, as these are inactive diagnoses.[^6]
4. Check off each active disease; check all that apply. Diagnoses listed in parentheses are examples and should not be considered all-inclusive lists — I6200 is not limited to the examples given (chronic bronchitis, restrictive lung diseases such as asbestosis).[^7]
5. If a disease or condition is not specifically listed in the checkbox items, enter the diagnosis and ICD code in item I8000, Additional active diagnosis.[^8]
6. If the primary medical condition is coded in I0020B, it must also be included in items I0100–I8000: Active Diagnoses in the Last 7 Days (relevant when the primary condition is coded 12, Debility, Cardiorespiratory Conditions, whose examples include COPD and asthma).[^9]
7. When Z codes are used for aftercare following hospitalization, another diagnosis for the related primary medical condition should be checked in items I0100–I7900 or entered in I8000.[^10]
8. Listing a disease/diagnosis on the resident's medical record problem list is not sufficient for determining active or inactive status; if a diagnosis/problem list is used, only diagnoses confirmed by the physician should be entered.[^11]
9. Monitoring of medications for adverse effects as part of usual nursing practice relates to management of pharmacotherapy and not to management or monitoring of the underlying disease, and therefore does not by itself establish active disease.[^12]
10. Where a practitioner has potentially misdiagnosed a resident with a condition for which there is a lack of appropriate diagnostic information in the medical record, the corresponding diagnosis in Section I should not be coded.[^13]

## What the chart must show

1. Physician-documented diagnosis (or by NP, PA, or CNS if allowable under state licensure laws) of asthma/COPD/chronic lung disease within the last 60 days; medical record sources include progress notes, most recent history and physical, transfer documents, discharge summaries, diagnosis/problem list. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^14]
   If a diagnosis/problem list is used, only diagnoses confirmed by the physician should be entered.
2. Diagnoses communicated verbally must be documented in the medical record by the physician; diagnostic information obtained from family members and close contacts must also be documented in the medical record by the physician. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^15]
3. Review medical record sources for the last 7 days to identify "active" diagnoses: transfer documents, physician progress notes, recent history and physical, recent discharge summaries, nursing assessments, nursing care plans, medication sheets, doctor's orders, consults and official diagnostic reports. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^16]
4. Specific documentation by a physician, nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or clinical nurse specialist that the condition is active is sufficient and requires no additional confirmation. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^17]
   Framed as an indicator that "may assist assessors" in determining active status.
5. In the absence of specific documentation that a disease is active, recent onset or acute exacerbation indicated by a positive study, test or procedure, hospitalization for acute symptoms, and/or recent change in therapy in the last 7 days may confirm active disease (sources: radiological reports, hospital discharge summaries, doctor's orders). *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^18]
   Indicator, not a mandatory documentation element.
6. Symptoms and abnormal signs indicating ongoing or decompensated disease in the last 7 days may confirm active disease; a symptom must be specifically attributed to the disease (sources: radiological reports, nursing assessments and care plans, progress notes). *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^19]
   "Therefore, a symptom must be specifically attributed to the disease."
7. Ongoing therapy with medications or other interventions to manage the condition requiring monitoring for therapeutic efficacy or for potentially severe side effects in the last 7 days indicates active disease. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^20]
   Applies to inhaled/oral pharmacotherapy managing an ongoing condition; monitoring solely for medication adverse effects as usual nursing practice does not count (p. I-14).

## ⚠️ Conflicts — please read carefully

The manual (or two documents) say different things here. Both sides are shown; nothing has been dropped.

> **Which look-back window governs coding of active diagnoses in I0100–I8000 (and thus I6200)**
>
> - *Federal manual:* Diagnosis identification (Step 1) uses a 60-day look-back period.[^21]
> - *Federal manual:* Diagnosis status (active or inactive, Step 2) uses a 7-day look-back period.[^1]
>
> Status: Resolved by the document: both windows apply sequentially — diagnosis must be physician-documented within 60 days AND active during the 7-day look-back period (p. I-9 coding instruction).

> **Cross-reference page for I2300 UTI coding instructions (affects the section-wide exception statement)**
>
> - *Federal manual:* "Please refer to Page I-13 for specific coding instructions for Item I2300 UTI."[^22]
> - *Federal manual:* "Please refer to Item I2300 UTI, Page I-14 for specific coding instructions."[^23]
>
> Status: unresolved — surface to user; does not affect I6200 coding (the UTI exception applies only to I2300).
