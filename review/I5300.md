## Look-back window

**7 days**[^1]

The section I0100–I8000 (which includes I5300, Parkinson's disease) has two stated look-back periods: diagnosis identification (Step 1) is a 60-day look-back for the physician-documented diagnosis, and diagnosis status active/inactive (Step 2) is a 7-day look-back. The assessor's observation window for determining active status is 7 days; the 60-day window applies to identifying that a physician-documented diagnosis exists. The 30-day exception stated in the manual applies only to Item I2300 UTI, not to I5300. The document does not state how the days are counted relative to the ARD on the supplied pages.

> **Note:** the manual states more than one time period for this item. See the *Conflicts* section below.

## Coding rules

1. I5300 is the Parkinson's disease item within the Neurological disease category of I0100–I8000, Active Diagnoses in the Last 7 Days.[^4]
2. Code diseases that have a documented diagnosis in the last 60 days and that have a direct relationship to the resident's current functional status, cognitive status, mood or behavior status, medical treatments, nursing monitoring, or risk of death during the 7-day look-back period.[^2]
3. The disease conditions in this section require a physician-documented diagnosis (or by a nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or clinical nurse specialist if allowable under state licensure laws) in the last 60 days.[^5]
4. Once a diagnosis is identified, it must be determined whether it is active; active diagnoses are those with a direct relationship to the resident's current functional, cognitive, or mood or behavior status, medical treatments, nursing monitoring, or risk of death during the 7-day look-back period.[^6]
5. Check off each active disease; check all that apply.[^7]
6. Conditions that have been resolved, do not affect the resident's current status, or do not drive the plan of care during the 7-day look-back period are inactive and are not coded.[^3]
7. Listing a disease/diagnosis on the resident's medical record problem list is not sufficient for determining active or inactive status; if a diagnosis/problem list is used as a source, only diagnoses confirmed by the physician should be entered.[^8]
8. Ongoing therapy with medications or other interventions in the last 7 days to manage the condition, where the medication requires monitoring for therapeutic efficacy or for potentially severe side effects, indicates active disease.[^9]
9. Routine nursing monitoring of all medications for adverse effects does not by itself establish active disease; for coding purposes that monitoring relates to management of pharmacotherapy, not management or monitoring of the underlying disease.[^10]
10. Where practitioners have potentially misdiagnosed a resident with a condition for which there is a lack of appropriate diagnostic information in the medical record, the corresponding Section I diagnosis should not be coded.[^11]
11. Examples of diseases listed for a category are not all-inclusive; diseases to be coded in these categories are not limited to those listed in the examples.[^12]
12. If a disease or condition is not specifically listed among the checkbox items, enter the diagnosis and ICD code in item I8000, Additional active diagnosis.[^13]
13. If a resident is receiving aftercare following hospitalization, a Z code may be assigned; when Z codes are used, another diagnosis for the related primary medical condition should be checked in items I0100–I7900 or entered in I8000.[^14]
14. If the primary medical condition coded in I0020B is Parkinson's disease (an example of code 06, Progressive Neurological Conditions), it must also be included in items I0100–I8000: Active Diagnoses in the Last 7 Days.[^15]
15. For a resident with severe physical disability and minimal ability for purposeful movement (functional quadriplegia), the primary physician-documented diagnosis should be coded on the MDS rather than the resulting paralysis or paresis — relevant when dementia related to Parkinson's disease or Parkinson's disease itself produces functional paralysis.[^16]

## What the chart must show

1. Physician-documented diagnosis of Parkinson's disease (or by a nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or clinical nurse specialist if allowable under state licensure laws) within the last 60 days. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^17]
   Step 1 of the two-step assessment process.
2. Medical record sources for the physician diagnosis include progress notes, the most recent history and physical, transfer documents, discharge summaries, diagnosis/problem list, and other resources; if a diagnosis/problem list is used, only diagnoses confirmed by the physician should be entered. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^18]
3. Diagnoses communicated verbally must be documented in the medical record by the physician; diagnostic information including past history obtained from family members and close contacts must also be documented in the medical record by the physician. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^19]
   Second half (family/close contacts) is a separate bullet on the same page.
4. Evidence of active status is checked in the medical record for the last 7 days using sources such as transfer documents, physician progress notes, recent history and physical, recent discharge summaries, nursing assessments, nursing care plans, medication sheets, doctor's orders, consults and official diagnostic reports. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^20]
5. Specific documentation by a physician, nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or clinical nurse specialist that the condition is active (found in progress notes, most recent history and physical, transfer notes, hospital discharge summary, etc.) is sufficient documentation of active disease and requires no additional confirmation. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^21]
   Framed as an indicator that may assist assessors; the manual states such documentation "would require no additional confirmation."
6. In the absence of specific documentation that the disease is active, documentation of recent onset or acute exacerbation (positive study, test or procedure, hospitalization for acute symptoms, and/or recent change in therapy in the last 7 days) may be used to confirm active disease; sources may include radiological reports, hospital discharge summaries, doctor's orders. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^22]
   Presented as an indicator that may be used.
7. Documentation of symptoms and abnormal signs indicating ongoing or decompensated disease in the last 7 days, with the symptom specifically attributed to the disease; sources may include radiological reports, nursing assessments and care plans, progress notes. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^23]
   The manual adds that a symptom must be specifically attributed to the disease.
8. Documentation of ongoing therapy with medications or other interventions in the last 7 days that requires monitoring for therapeutic efficacy or for potentially severe side effects (e.g., progress notes, doctor's orders for medications, documentation of physical or other therapy for functional limitations caused by the condition). *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^24]

## ⚠️ Conflicts — please read carefully

The manual (or two documents) say different things here. Both sides are shown; nothing has been dropped.

> **Two look-back windows coexist for Section I diagnoses (60-day identification vs. 7-day active status)**
>
> - *Federal manual:* Diagnosis identification (Step 1) uses a 60-day look-back period for the physician-documented diagnosis.[^25]
> - *Federal manual:* Diagnosis status (active or inactive, Step 2) uses a 7-day look-back period.[^1]
>
> Status: Resolved by the document itself: it explicitly assigns the 60-day window to Step 1 (identifying the physician-documented diagnosis) and the 7-day window to Step 2 (determining active status). The 7-day window is the observation window for determining whether I5300 is coded.
