## Look-back window

**7 days**[^1]

The item's own section states two look-back periods: diagnosis identification (Step 1) uses a 60-day look-back ("The disease conditions in this section require a physician-documented diagnosis ... in the last 60 days", p. I-8), and diagnosis status Active/Inactive (Step 2) uses a 7-day look-back (p. I-8). The 7-day period is the observation window against which active status is judged and is recorded as the look-back window; the 60-day diagnosis-identification window is the second stated window. Per the general convention (p. 2-21) the ARD is the last day of the observation period, so the 7-day window is the ARD plus the 6 previous calendar days. The 30-day exception stated on p. I-14 applies to Item I2300 UTI only, not to I5300.

> **Note:** the manual states more than one time period for this item. See the *Conflicts* section below.

## Coding rules

1. I5300 is the Parkinson's disease item within the Neurological category of I0100–I8000, Active Diagnoses in the Last 7 Days.[^4]
2. I5300 is a check-box item completed under the instruction "Check all that apply"; diagnoses listed in parentheses in the item set are examples and are not all-inclusive lists.[^5]
3. Code the disease only if it has a documented diagnosis in the last 60 days AND has a direct relationship to the resident's current functional status, cognitive status, mood or behavior status, medical treatments, nursing monitoring, or risk of death during the 7-day look-back period.[^6]
4. The diagnosis must be physician-documented (or documented by a nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or clinical nurse specialist if allowable under state licensure laws) within the last 60 days.[^7]
5. Do not code conditions that have been resolved, do not affect the resident's current status, or do not drive the resident's plan of care during the 7-day look-back period; these are inactive diagnoses.[^3]
6. If a diagnosis/problem list is used as a source, only diagnoses confirmed by the physician should be entered.[^8]
7. Listing a disease/diagnosis on the resident's medical record problem list is not sufficient for determining active or inactive status.[^9]
8. In the absence of specific physician documentation that the disease is active, active disease may be confirmed by recent onset or acute exacerbation indicated by a positive study, test or procedure, hospitalization for acute symptoms, and/or recent change in therapy in the last 7 days.[^10]
9. In the absence of specific documentation that the disease is active, symptoms and abnormal signs indicating ongoing or decompensated disease in the last 7 days may confirm active disease; a symptom must be specifically attributed to the disease.[^11]
10. Ongoing therapy with medications or other interventions in the last 7 days to manage a condition that requires monitoring for therapeutic efficacy or for potentially severe side effects indicates active disease; if a medication is prescribed for a condition requiring regular staff monitoring of the drug's effect on that condition, the prescription indicates active disease.[^12]
11. Monitoring of medications for adverse effects as usual nursing practice relates to management of pharmacotherapy, not to management or monitoring of the underlying disease, and therefore does not by itself establish active disease.[^13]
12. Where practitioners have potentially misdiagnosed a resident with a condition for which appropriate diagnostic information is lacking in the medical record, the corresponding Section I diagnosis should not be coded.[^14]
13. Dementia related to Parkinson's disease is listed as an example of Non-Alzheimer's Dementia and is coded at I4800, not at I5300.[^15]
14. If the resident has no active diagnoses from the listed items within the last 7 days, check I7900, None of the above active diagnoses.[^16]
15. If a disease or condition is not specifically listed among the check-box items, enter the diagnosis and ICD code in item I8000, Additional active diagnosis.[^17]
16. When the primary medical condition coded in I0020B applies, it must also be included in items I0100–I8000, Active Diagnoses in the Last 7 Days.[^18]

## What the chart must show

1. Physician (or NP/PA/CNS if allowable under state licensure laws) documentation of the Parkinson's disease diagnosis in the medical record within the last 60 days; medical record sources include progress notes, most recent history and physical, transfer documents, discharge summaries, and diagnosis/problem list. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^19]
   Diagnosis identification step (60-day window).
2. Diagnoses communicated verbally by the physician must be documented in the medical record by the physician. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^20]
3. Diagnostic information, including past history obtained from family members and close contacts, must be documented in the medical record by the physician. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^21]
4. Review the following medical record sources for the last 7 days to identify active diagnoses: transfer documents, physician progress notes, recent history and physical, recent discharge summaries, nursing assessments, nursing care plans, medication sheets, doctor's orders, consults and official diagnostic reports. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^22]
   Active-status step (7-day window).
5. Specific documentation by a physician, nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or clinical nurse specialist that the condition is active (found in progress notes, most recent history and physical, transfer notes, hospital discharge summary, etc.) is sufficient and requires no additional confirmation. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^23]
6. When active status is inferred rather than stated, supporting sources may include radiological reports, hospital discharge summaries, doctor's orders, nursing assessments and care plans, and progress notes documenting treatment, medications, or therapy during the 7-day look-back period. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^24]
   The manual frames these as indicators/examples that may assist assessors.

## ⚠️ Conflicts — please read carefully

The manual (or two documents) say different things here. Both sides are shown; nothing has been dropped.

> **Two coexisting look-back windows for Section I active diagnoses (60 days for diagnosis identification vs. 7 days for active status)**
>
> - *Federal manual:* Diagnosis identification (Step 1) uses a 60-day look-back period.[^25]
> - *Federal manual:* Diagnosis status (active or inactive, Step 2) uses a 7-day look-back period.[^1]
>
> Status: Resolved by the document itself: both windows apply in sequence — the diagnosis must be physician-documented within 60 days and must be active during the 7-day look-back period (p. I-9). The 7-day period is recorded as the observation/look-back window for coding I5300.
