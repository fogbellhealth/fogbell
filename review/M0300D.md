## Coding rules

1. Stage 4 pressure ulcer is defined as full thickness tissue loss with exposed bone, tendon or muscle; slough or eschar may be present on some parts of the wound bed, and it often includes undermining and tunneling.[^3]
2. For coding purposes, determine that the lesion being assessed is primarily related to pressure and that other conditions have been ruled out; if pressure is not the primary cause, do not code here.[^4]
3. M0300D1: Enter the number of pressure ulcers currently present whose deepest anatomical stage is Stage 4.[^1]
4. M0300D1: Enter 0 if no Stage 4 pressure ulcers are present and skip to M0300E, Unstageable – Non-removable dressing.[^5]
5. M0300D2: Enter the number of these Stage 4 pressure ulcers first noted at Stage 4 at the time of admission/entry AND, for residents reentering after a hospital stay, those acquired during the hospitalization (i.e., not acquired in the nursing facility prior to admission to the hospital).[^6]
6. M0300D2: Enter 0 if no Stage 4 pressure ulcers were first noted at the time of admission/entry or reentry.[^7]
7. Pressure ulcers with exposed cartilage are classified as Stage 4, because cartilage serves the same anatomical function as bone.[^8]
8. In a Stage 4 pressure ulcer, exposed bone/tendon/muscle is visible or directly palpable.[^9]
9. Depth of a Stage 4 pressure ulcer varies by anatomical location; ulcers on the bridge of the nose, ear, occiput, and malleolus can be shallow because these locations lack subcutaneous tissue.[^10]
10. Measurement of tunneling and undermining is not recorded on the MDS (though it should be assessed, monitored and treated as part of the comprehensive care plan).[^11]
11. Determine the deepest anatomical stage for each pressure ulcer; at admission, code based on the first skin assessment conducted on or after and as close to admission as possible. Do not reverse or back stage. Consider current and historical levels of tissue involvement.[^12]
12. If a stageable pressure ulcer has ever been classified at a higher numerical stage than what is observed now, it continues to be classified at the higher numerical stage until healed, unless it becomes unstageable.[^13]
13. A previously closed pressure ulcer that opens again should be reported at its worst stage, unless currently presenting at a higher stage or unstageable.[^14]
14. Present on admission determination: if a pressure ulcer/injury was present on admission/entry or reentry and subsequently increased in numerical stage during the stay, it is coded at the higher stage and that higher stage is not considered "present on admission."[^15]
15. If a pressure ulcer/injury was unstageable on admission/entry or reentry and later becomes numerically stageable, it is considered "present on admission" at the stage at which it first becomes numerically stageable; a subsequent increase in numerical stage is not coded as present on admission.[^16]
16. If a resident with a pressure ulcer/injury is hospitalized and the ulcer/injury increases in numerical stage during the hospitalization, it is coded as "present on admission" upon reentry.[^17]
17. Once an unstageable ulcer covered with slough/eschar is debrided such that the anatomic depth of soft tissue damage can be determined (e.g., bone visible), code the ulcer at the reclassified stage (example reclassified to Stage 4 and coded at M0300D1/M0300D2).[^18]

## What the chart must show

1. Perform a head-to-toe/full body skin assessment focusing on bony prominences and pressure-bearing areas (sacrum, buttocks, heels, ankles, etc.) to identify all Stage 4 pressure ulcers currently present. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^19]
   Listed as Step 1 of Steps for Assessment for M0300D.
2. Identify the number of these Stage 4 pressure ulcers that were present on admission/entry or reentry. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^20]
   Step 4 of Steps for Assessment for M0300D.
3. Review the history of each pressure ulcer in the medical record; careful documentation and tracking of pressure ulcers supports accurate coding of this item. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^21]
   From Step 1 (Determine Deepest Anatomical Stage) for completing M0300A–G.
4. Review the medical record for the history of the ulcer/injury and for its location and stage at the time of admission/entry or reentry to determine "present on admission." *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^22]
   From Step 3: Determine "Present on Admission" for M0300A–G.
