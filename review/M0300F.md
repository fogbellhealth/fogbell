## Coding rules

1. M0300F1: Enter the number of pressure ulcers that are unstageable related to slough and/or eschar.[^1]
2. M0300F1: Enter 0 if no unstageable pressure ulcers related to slough and/or eschar are present, and skip to M0300G, Unstageable – Deep tissue injury.[^3]
3. M0300F2: Enter the number of these unstageable slough/eschar pressure ulcers first noted at the time of admission/entry AND, for residents reentering after a hospital stay, those acquired during the hospitalization (i.e., not acquired in the nursing facility prior to hospital admission).[^4]
4. M0300F2: Enter 0 if no unstageable pressure ulcers related to slough and/or eschar were first noted at the time of admission/entry or reentry.[^5]
5. Pressure ulcers covered with slough and/or eschar where the wound bed cannot be visualized are coded as unstageable, because the true anatomic depth of soft tissue damage (and therefore stage) cannot be determined.[^6]
6. Once the pressure ulcer is debrided of slough and/or eschar such that the anatomic depth of soft tissue damage can be determined, code the ulcer for the reclassified stage; the ulcer does not have to be completely debrided or free of all slough/eschar for reclassification of stage to occur.[^7]
7. Determine the number of pressure ulcers that are unstageable due to slough and/or eschar, and identify the number of these that were present on admission/entry or reentry using the Step 3 'Present on Admission' process.[^8]
8. If the wound bed is only partially covered by eschar or slough and the anatomical depth of tissue damage can be visualized or palpated, numerically stage the ulcer and do not code it as unstageable.[^9]
9. If, after careful cleansing, the pressure ulcer's/injury's anatomical tissues are obscured such that the extent of soft tissue damage cannot be observed or palpated, the pressure ulcer/injury is considered unstageable.[^10]
10. A numerically stageable pressure ulcer/injury that was present on admission/entry or reentry and becomes unstageable due to slough or eschar during the stay is coded at M0300F and should not be coded as 'present on admission.'[^11]
11. If a pressure ulcer/injury was unstageable on admission/entry or reentry and then becomes numerically stageable later, it is considered 'present on admission' at the stage at which it first becomes numerically stageable; a subsequent increase in numerical stage is not coded as 'present on admission.'[^12]
12. If a resident is hospitalized and the ulcer/injury becomes unstageable due to slough or eschar during the hospitalization, it should be coded as 'present on admission' upon reentry.[^13]
13. If a pressure ulcer was numerically staged, became unstageable, and is subsequently debrided sufficiently to be numerically staged, compare its numerical stage before and after; if the numerical stage has increased, code the ulcer as not 'present on admission.'[^14]
14. If a pressure ulcer/injury was unstageable on admission/entry or reentry and then becomes unstageable for another reason, it is considered 'present on admission' at the new unstageable status.[^15]
15. Example: a resident admitted with a sacral pressure ulcer 100% covered with black eschar is coded M0300F1 as 1 and M0300F2 as 1, present on admission/entry or reentry.[^16]
16. Example: an ulcer admitted as a Stage 2 that later becomes covered with slough is coded M0300F1 as 1 and M0300F2 as 0, not present on admission/entry or reentry, because it can no longer be coded as a Stage 2.[^17]
17. Example: an unstageable ulcer due to eschar that is debrided and first numerically staged is coded at that stage as present on admission/entry or reentry, and any remaining unstageable eschar ulcer continues to be coded at M0300F1/F2.[^18]

## What the chart must show

1. Medical record documentation of the pressure ulcer's history, location, and stage at the time of admission/entry or reentry to support the 'present on admission' coding at M0300F2. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^19]
   Step 3 'Determine Present on Admission' process referenced by M0300F Steps for Assessment (see p. M-23).
2. Documentation/tracking of each pressure ulcer's history in the medical record; nursing homes that carefully document and track pressure ulcers will be able to more accurately code this item. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^20]
   From the shared M0300 Step 1 instructions.
3. Documentation of consultation with the resident's physician, nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or clinical nurse specialist before removal of stable eschar on the heels, including ruling out ischemia. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^21]
   Framed as a clinical-practice caution in Coding Tips rather than as an MDS coding requirement.
