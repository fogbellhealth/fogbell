## Look-back window

**7 days**[^1]

Two look-back periods apply to this section: diagnosis identification (Step 1) uses a 60-day look-back for a physician-documented diagnosis, and diagnosis status (active/inactive, Step 2) uses a 7-day look-back. the look-back window is set to the 7-day active-diagnosis observation window; the 60-day diagnosis-identification window is recorded here and as a conflict entry. The exception for I2300 UTI does not apply to I6300.

> **Note:** the manual states more than one time period for this item. See the *Conflicts* section below.

## Coding rules

1. I6300 is the Pulmonary-category item for respiratory failure within I0100–I8000: Active Diagnoses in the Last 7 Days.[^4]
2. Code diseases that have a documented diagnosis in the last 60 days and have a direct relationship to the resident's current functional status, cognitive status, mood or behavior status, medical treatments, nursing monitoring, or risk of death during the 7-day look-back period.[^2]
3. The disease conditions in this section require a physician-documented diagnosis (or by a nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or clinical nurse specialist if allowable under state licensure laws) in the last 60 days.[^5]
4. Do not code conditions that have been resolved, do not affect the resident's current status, or do not drive the resident's plan of care during the 7-day look-back period; these are inactive diagnoses.[^6]
5. Check off each active disease; check all that apply.[^7]
6. Examples of diseases listed for a category are not exhaustive; diseases coded in these categories are not limited to those listed in the examples.[^8]
7. If a disease or condition is not specifically listed, enter the diagnosis and ICD code in item I8000, Additional active diagnosis.[^9]
8. If the primary medical condition coded in I0020B is respiratory failure, it must also be included (checked) in items I0100–I8000.[^10]
9. When Z codes are used for aftercare following a hospitalization, another diagnosis for the related primary medical condition should be checked in items I0100–I7900 or entered in I8000.[^11]
10. Listing a disease/diagnosis on the resident's medical record problem list is not sufficient for determining active or inactive status.[^12]
11. Monitoring of medications for adverse effects as usual nursing practice relates to management of pharmacotherapy, not to management or monitoring of the underlying disease, for coding purposes.[^13]
12. Where practitioners have potentially misdiagnosed a resident with a condition lacking appropriate diagnostic information in the medical record, the corresponding Section I diagnosis should not be coded.[^14]

## What the chart must show

1. Physician-documented diagnosis of respiratory failure (or by nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or clinical nurse specialist if allowable under state licensure laws) within the last 60 days, found in sources such as progress notes, most recent history and physical, transfer documents, discharge summaries, or diagnosis/problem list (only physician-confirmed entries on a problem list). *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^15]
   Step 1 requirement for all Section I diagnosis items.
2. Diagnoses communicated verbally must be documented in the medical record by the physician. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^16]
3. Diagnostic information, including past history obtained from family members and close contacts, must be documented in the medical record by the physician. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^17]
4. Evidence of active status within the last 7 days from medical record sources: transfer documents, physician progress notes, recent history and physical, recent discharge summaries, nursing assessments, nursing care plans, medication sheets, doctor's orders, consults and official diagnostic reports. *(importance to an auditor: high)*[^18]
5. Specific documentation by a physician, nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or clinical nurse specialist that the condition is active is sufficient and requires no additional confirmation. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^19]
   Framed as an indicator that may assist assessors.
6. In the absence of specific documentation that the disease is active: documentation of recent onset or acute exacerbation indicated by a positive study, test or procedure, hospitalization for acute symptoms, and/or recent change in therapy in the last 7 days (sources: radiological reports, hospital discharge summaries, doctor's orders). *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^20]
7. In the absence of specific documentation that the disease is active: documented symptoms and abnormal signs indicating ongoing or decompensated disease in the last 7 days, with the symptom specifically attributed to the disease. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^21]
8. In the absence of specific documentation that the disease is active: documentation of ongoing therapy with medications or other interventions to manage the condition requiring monitoring for therapeutic efficacy or for potentially severe side effects in the last 7 days. *(importance to an auditor: medium)*[^22]
